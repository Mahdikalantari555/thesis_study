## Summary
Review of the physiological basis of drought tolerance framed as a scenario-dependent probabilistic approach rather than a fixed set of traits. The authors distinguish short-term feedbacks (stomatal control, hydraulic conductance, osmotic adjustment) from long-term developmental traits, and argue that trait ranking depends on drought scenario (mild versus severe deficit). They propose integrating high-throughput phenotyping, multi-site trials, crop modeling, and genomic prediction to obtain probabilistic, environment-conditioned drought tolerance estimates. This framework challenges the use of global static stress indices and supports context-dependent labeling in crop stress monitoring.

## Methods / Approach
- Literature synthesis of short-term feedbacks (stomata, hydraulics, osmotic adjustment) and long-term drought-response traits
- Scenario-dependent (mild vs. severe deficit) conceptual modeling of trait expression
- Advocacy of combined phenotyping + multi-site trials + mechanistic modeling + genomic prediction for probabilistic drought tolerance

## Relevance to thesis
The scenario-dependent probabilistic framing directly supports probabilistic or mixture labels for crop stress classes rather than static binary CWSI thresholds. Multi-scale temporal feedbacks—rapid stomatal response versus slower osmotic and developmental adjustment—justify long-sequence models (e.g., Mamba) over single-date indices. For Khuzestan irrigated systems, the warning against transferring global static stress indices aligns with the need for environment- and crop-specific calibration under salinity and heat co-stress. The multi-site, multi-level evidence framework also motivates multi-temporal and multi-sensor fusion designs. Low Earth-observation and ML content means the paper is used mainly for label philosophy and temporal-model justification, not as an RS pipeline source.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Low EO/ML content; primarily conceptual and physiological
- Warn against applying global static stress indices across diverse environments and drought scenarios
- Opportunities to operationalize probabilistic stress labels in multi-temporal deep sequence models
