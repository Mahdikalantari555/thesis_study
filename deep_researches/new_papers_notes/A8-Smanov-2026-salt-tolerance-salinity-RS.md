## Summary
Bibliometric and thematic review (58 Scopus studies, 1994–2024) of how crop salt tolerance mediates spectral–soil salinity relationships in irrigated agroecosystems. Existing RS salinity models are surface-oriented, bulk electrical conductivity (ECe)-based, and typically ignore crop-specific thresholds, root-zone salinity, ion composition, and phenology. The authors argue for soil–plant–water–spectral integrative models that explicitly incorporate crop salt tolerance as a mediating variable. This reframes RS salinity mapping from a pure soil-chemistry problem to a coupled soil–plant system problem.

## Methods / Approach
- Bibliometric analysis of Scopus-indexed literature (n = 58, 1994–2024)
- Thematic synthesis of RS-based soil salinity mapping approaches and their treatment (or neglect) of crop salt tolerance
- Critical gap analysis: surface orientation, bulk ECe reliance, crop-specific thresholds, root-zone dynamics, ion composition, phenology
- Proposal of soil–plant–water–spectral integrative modeling framework

## Key quantitative results
- ~1 billion ha of land salinized globally (~20% of irrigated land) (review-cited)
- Bibliometric corpus: n = 58 Scopus studies spanning 1994–2024
- Dominant model class: surface-oriented, bulk ECe, with crop tolerance and ion composition largely ignored

## Relevance to thesis
This is the highest-priority salinity framing reference for the thesis core setting: irrigated, arid Khuzestan with concurrent water deficit and salt stress. Ion composition confounders directly motivate caution when interpreting spectral or thermal signatures as pure water-stress proxies. The call for crop-specific, multi-phase, soil–plant–water integrative models matches the thesis multi-temporal deep sequence design better than static single-date salinity indices. Kazakh and Xinjiang irrigated analogs transfer conceptually to Khuzestan's arid irrigated agroecosystems. Crop salt tolerance as a mediating variable provides a physiological basis for multi-class or probabilistic stress labels under salinity–drought co-occurrence.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Multi-temporal, crop-specific salt response models are largely absent from the reviewed literature
- Remote disentanglement of salt versus water stress remains an open caveat requiring multi-sensor, multi-temporal approaches
- Soil–plant–water–spectral integrative models proposed but not operationally validated at scale
- Kazakh / Xinjiang analog transfers strengthen the arid-irrigated framing for Khuzestan
