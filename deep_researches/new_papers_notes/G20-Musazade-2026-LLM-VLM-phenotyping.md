## Summary
This review examines the integration of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) in plant stress phenotyping, spanning trait recognition, knowledge extraction, and decision support. It highlights multimodal reasoning and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and mentions Mamba-based encoders as an emerging architecture. Challenges include data quality, generalization, transparency, and equity. Background context from the literature: biotic stresses cause 17–40% yield loss and abiotic stresses up to 50%.

## Methods / Architecture
- Review of LLM and VLM applications across the phenotyping pipeline: trait recognition → knowledge extraction → decision support.
- Emphasis on multimodal reasoning and RAG.
- Mentions Mamba-based encoders as relevant architectural directions.

## Key quantitative results
- Review paper; background statistics cited: biotic stresses 17–40% yield loss; abiotic stresses up to 50% yield loss.
- No primary experimental benchmark in the first-page extract.

## Relevance to thesis
Bridges plant stress phenotyping and foundation-model-style multimodal agents: provides the conceptual link between EO-FM embeddings, VLM reasoning, and decision-support agents for crop stress. Mamba mentions align with exploration of efficient sequence models for time-series or multi-band RS data.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Review spans controlled and field phenotyping; EO-scale arid monitoring coverage is limited.
- Active learning loops and label-efficient adaptation are not central themes.
- Opportunity: position the thesis at the intersection of EO foundation models + LLM/VLM decision agents + AL for arid stress.
