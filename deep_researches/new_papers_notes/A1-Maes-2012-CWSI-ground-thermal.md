## Summary
Canonical review of ground-based thermal remote sensing for estimating evapotranspiration (ET) and drought stress in agriculture. It covers leaf- and canopy-scale energy balance theory, sensitivity to weather and canopy structure, and a full taxonomy of stress indicators including stress time, stress degree day, crop water stress index (CWSI; analytical, empirical, and direct forms), stomatal conductance indices, and the 3T method. The paper evaluates performance, assumptions, and practical challenges of each technique. Scope is limited to ground techniques (infrared thermometers and thermal cameras); satellite platforms are out of scope. Despite being ground-based, the theoretical CWSI formulations remain the reference framework for thermal stress indices derived from spaceborne sensors.

## Methods / Approach
- Theoretical modeling of canopy temperature (Ts) and energy-balance formulations at leaf and canopy scales
- Systematic survey of ground-based thermal techniques (IR thermometers, thermal cameras)
- Explicit development and comparison of CTD (canopy-to-air temperature difference) and CWSI formulations (analytical, empirical, and direct thermal approaches)
- Sensitivity analyses with respect to meteorological variables, canopy architecture, and stomatal conductance drivers
- Evaluation of stomatal conductance indices and multi-thermal (3T) methods

## Key quantitative results
- No single headline accuracy figure in the first-page extract; method equations for CWSI/CTD are provided
- Sensitivity analyses document dependence of thermal indices on weather, canopy structure, and boundary-layer conductance

## Relevance to thesis
This paper anchors the CWSI concept as both a ground-truth reference and an auxiliary feature class for multi-temporal crop stress monitoring in Khuzestan. It establishes the physical bridge required to transfer ground CWSI formulations to Landsat-derived canopy temperature proxies. The review also notes that osmotic (salinity-induced) stomatal effects can mimic drought Ts responses, directly motivating mixed-stress disentanglement in irrigated arid systems. For the thesis, CWSI serves as the canonical thermal stress baseline against which deep sequence models and multi-sensor fusion approaches are compared. Ground techniques remain out of scope for operational monitoring, so the key transfer is the CWSI theory, not the measurement hardware.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Explicit satellite bridge from ground CWSI to Landsat TIRS-derived proxies remains underdeveloped in the reviewed literature
- Pure water deficit versus salinity-induced stomatal effects are not cleanly separated in classical CWSI formulations
- Opportunities for integrating CWSI as a physically constrained feature with optical SSL and multi-temporal sequence models (e.g., Mamba)
