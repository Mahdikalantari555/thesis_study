## Summary
Methodological review of ~20 years of remote sensing for broadacre agriculture, covering crop productivity estimation, phenology monitoring, and environmental stress detection. Vegetation indices (VIs) track yield reasonably well; phenology advances improve forecasting; thermal and multispectral sensors detect abiotic and biotic stresses but with limited stress specificity. The authors call for multi-sensor and multi-scale fusion and tighter RS–crop model assimilation to improve resilience in broadacre systems.

## Methods / Approach
- Methodological literature review spanning ~20 years of RS for broadacre agriculture
- Thematic organization: productivity (VIs and yield), phenology, environmental (abiotic/biotic) stress detection
- Synthesis of multi-sensor / multi-scale fusion and RS–crop model assimilation recommendations

## Key quantitative results
- Broadacre systems constitute ~¾ of global cropland (review-cited)
- Western Australia grain-belt profitability has declined ~22% since 2000 (review-cited)
- Heat and drought account for ~20–49% of yield variability (review-cited ranges)
- Stress specificity of thermal/multispectral detection remains limited despite adequate detection capability

## Relevance to thesis
This paper frames the multi-stress specificity limit that the thesis explicitly targets: detection is feasible, but separating concurrent stresses remains unresolved. The thesis pitch for irrigated arid systems (Khuzestan) contrasts with the rainfed broadacre context while inheriting the same specificity challenge under heat and drought co-drivers. RS–crop model assimilation and multi-sensor fusion recommendations align with the thesis multi-temporal sequence and thermal-optical fusion design. Review-cited quantitative ranges on heat/drought yield impact provide motivation numbers, though they should be verified against primary sources before formal proposal use.

## Gaps / opportunities
