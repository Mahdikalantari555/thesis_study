## Summary
Critical review of crop water stress assessment with remote sensing. Links relative water content (RWC) to equivalent water thickness (EWT) and soil moisture as measurable stress proxies, then reviews evapotranspiration and sun-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) in relation to crop water stress. Closes with a taxonomy of sensing technologies: optical, thermometric, land-surface-temperature, multispectral (spaceborne/airborne), hyperspectral, and LiDAR (incl. Sentinel-1).

## Methods / Approach
- Narrative/critical review organized by measurable stress parameter (RWC → EWT → soil moisture)
- ET and SIF treated as process-level stress indicators
- Sensor-family taxonomy from proximal to satellite scale

## Key quantitative results
- No headline model numbers (review); provides the RWC–EWT–CWSI conceptual chain used by later empirical papers

## Relevance to thesis
Canonical reference for the water-deficit branch of the thesis (concurrent water–salinity disentanglement). Supplies the proxy definitions (RWC/EWT/CWSI) and the sensor taxonomy that the Khuzestan sugarcane feature design can cite directly. Complements Maes & Steppe 2012 (ground thermal) and Gerhards 2019 (TIR physics) already in the repo.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Review predates foundation models, SSL, and Mamba-style sequence models — supports the claim that label-efficient temporal architectures are absent from the water-stress RS canon
- No treatment of salinity co-stress — the review stays single-stress
