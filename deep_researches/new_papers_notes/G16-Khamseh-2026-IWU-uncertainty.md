# Uncertainty and performance of remote sensing-based irrigation water use estimates

**Authors:** Khamseh, F., & Danesh-Yazdi, M.  
**Year:** 2026  
**Venue:** Agricultural Water Management, 331, 110444  
**DOI / arXiv:** 10.1016/j.agwat.2026.110444  
**Local PDF:** 1-s2.0-S0378377426003252-main.pdf  
**Type:** methods  
**Thesis relevance:** MEDIUM  

## Summary
The study quantifies uncertainty and performance of remote sensing–based irrigation water use (IWU) estimates. IWU is derived as a residual from an irrigation-free water balance and compared across ET products: OpenET at 30 m versus MOD16 and PML at 500 m over Arizona in 2017. Root-zone soil moisture (RZSM) performance, product biases, contribution of ET residuals to total IWU, and coefficient of variation are reported.

## Methods / Architecture
- Residual IWU from an irrigation-free water balance.
- ET products: OpenET (30 m), MOD16 (500 m), PML (500 m).
- Study area: Arizona; year: 2017.
- Uncertainty assessment of RS-based IWU estimates.

## Key quantitative results
- RZSM: RMSE = 0.011 m³/m³, R² = 0.68.
- IWU bias ranges: OpenET −16–23%, MOD16 −93–94%, PML −55–60%.
- More than 97% of IWU derived from the ET residual.
- Coefficient of variation (CV) = 0.25.

## Relevance to thesis
Informs ET resolution and product-choice lessons for arid EO data quality: high-resolution OpenET substantially outperforms coarser MOD16/PML for residual-based water accounting, which matters when assembling covariates for crop-stress monitoring in arid regions.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Single-year, single-state (Arizona 2017) evaluation limits generalization.
- Focus is water-use estimation, not stress classification.
- Opportunity: propagate ET-product uncertainty into stress-detection confidence scores for agentic decision support.
