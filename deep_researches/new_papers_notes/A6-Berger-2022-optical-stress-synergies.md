## Summary
Systematic review of multi-sensor spectral synergies for crop stress detection and monitoring in the optical domain, covering 96 studies. The paper builds a stress-factor × stress-phase × plant-response framework mapped onto VIS, NIR, SWIR, TIR, and SIF spectral domains. Most studies still rely on single-source spectral proxies rather than true multi-sensor fusion. The authors recommend simultaneous multi-response analysis, multi-domain radiative transfer model (RTM) plus machine learning pipelines, and trait assimilation / Digital Twin approaches for operational crop stress monitoring.

## Methods / Approach
- Systematic review of 96 peer studies on multi-sensor optical crop stress RS
- Structured coding by stress factor, stress phase, and plant response across VIS/NIR/SWIR/TIR/SIF
- Gap analysis of single-source versus multi-sensor fusion designs
- Forward-looking synthesis of RTM+ML, trait assimilation, and Digital Twin workflows

## Key quantitative results
- n = 96 studies systematically reviewed
- Most studies still use single-source spectral proxies (no true multi-sensor simultaneous analysis)
- Multi-domain RTM + ML and Digital Twin frameworks recommended as future directions

## Relevance to thesis
This is the strongest RS positioning reference among the Agricultural cluster for the thesis multi-temporal, multi-sensor crop stress design. Deep sequence models (Mamba) and self-supervised learning (SSL) are largely absent from the 96-paper corpus, identifying a clear methodological gap the thesis can occupy. Concurrent-stress disentanglement is framed conceptually but not operationalized in reviewed pipelines—matching the thesis focus on mixed salinity/water/heat stress in irrigated arid systems. The stress-factor × stress-phase × plant-response taxonomy provides a ready organizing framework for Khuzestan case studies and feature design. Recommend promoting this review to Phase-1 citations as a landmark paper.

## Gaps / opportunities
