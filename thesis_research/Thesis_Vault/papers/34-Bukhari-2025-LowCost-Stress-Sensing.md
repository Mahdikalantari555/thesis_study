# 34 — Low-Cost Sensing for Early Stress and Disease Detection in Avocado (Bukhari et al., 2025)

**Full Citation:** Bukhari, A., Mamo, B., Hossain, M.S., Zhang, Z., Karimi, M., Enright, D., Manosalva, P., & Kim, H. (2025). Low-Cost Sensing and Classification for Early Stress and Disease Detection in Avocado Plants. *arXiv:2508.13379*.  
**Status:** Read

---

## What It Does

Evaluation of low-cost sensors (leaf spectral, soil EC, temperature) for early stress/disease detection in avocado plants in field conditions.

## Key Findings

- **Leaf spectral measurements**: Statistically significant when combined with ML
- **Soil data analysis**: Two-level hierarchical classifier achieving 75-86% accuracy
- **Temperature/conductivity**: Unreliable in field conditions due to environmental interference
- **Edge deployment**: Validated on embedded device with reasonable computational efficiency

## Stressors Detected

- Salinity stress (soil EC measurements)
- Phytophthora root rot (PRR)
- Multiple avocado genotypes

## Relevance to Thesis

**Low** — Multi-modal sensor fusion inspiration:
- ✅ Demonstrates value of combining leaf spectral + soil data
- ✅ Addresses real-world deployment challenges
- ⚠️ Ground-based sensors, not satellite
- ❌ No temporal time series component
- ❌ Different crop system (avocado vs. sugarcane)
- ⚠️ Could inspire multimodal fusion strategy (spectral + soil moisture + thermal)

## Key Takeaway

Low-cost sensor fusion can achieve meaningful accuracy for early stress detection, but sensor placement and environmental interference must be carefully managed. For your satellite-based approach, similar considerations apply to spatial resolution and atmospheric effects.
