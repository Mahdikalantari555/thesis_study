ABSTRACT The study of multi-temporal satellite data for agricultural land segmentation faces significant
computational challenges when processing extended temporal sequences, particularly due to CNNs’ limited
receptive fields and Transformers’ quadratic complexity, since convolutional neural networks are constrained
by local receptive fields, whereas Transformers experience quadratic complexity in their self-attention
mechanisms. These constraints particularly impede the precise identification of spectrally analogous crop
varieties and minority classes within agricultural landscapes. We present GWSC-SegMamba, an innovative
hybrid architecture that integrates State Space Models (SSMs) with Gate Wavelet Convolution (GWC) and
Gate Spatial Convolution (GSC) components to tackle multi-temporal agricultural segmentation issues. The
GWC component conducts multi-resolution analysis with discrete wavelet transforms to address spatial
resolution constraints for medium-resolution satellite data, whereas the GSC component identifies spatial
correlations essential for delineating crop boundaries. Our thorough assessment of three benchmark datasets
(Munich, Lombardia, and PASTIS) reveals substantial performance enhancements: a 7.65% increase in
mIoU relative to the conventional SegMamba, a 4.43% improvement over the Swin-UNETR baseline,
and an impressive 53.13% augmentation in IoU for difficult minority classes, including winter triticale.
The design processes temporal sequences with linear computational cost, incurring about 8-10% additional
computational overhead relative to the basic SegMamba and demonstrating enhanced scalability properties
compared to transformer-based approaches. These findings provide enhanced crop monitoring systems vital
for precision agriculture, especially in differentiating spectrally analogous crop varieties needed for yield
estimation and sustainable land management decisions.

II. RELATED WORK
The intersection of remote sensing technology and precision

A. APPLICATIONS OF REMOTE SENSING IN
AGRICUL TURAL MONITORING
Recent developments in satellite-based agricultural mon-
itoring have demonstrated the critical importance of
multi-temporal analysis for accurate crop identification and
yield estimation [32]. Environmental modeling approaches
using remote sensing data have evolved from simple vege-
tation index calculations to sophisticated machine learning
frameworks that can capture complex temporal patterns
in crop phenology [33]. The integration of multisensor
data fusion techniques has shown particular promise for
addressing the spatial and temporal resolution limitations
inherent in individual satellite systems [1].
The advancement of precision agriculture applications
has been significantly enhanced by the availability of
high-temporal-resolution satellite data, particularly from the
Sentinel constellation, which provides the multi-temporal
observations necessary for distinguishing crops with
similar spectral characteristics but different phenological
patterns [2]. These developments have established the
foundation for automated agricultural monitoring systems
that can support decision-making processes at both the field
and regional scales.

B. DEEP LEARNING METHODOLOGIES FOR
MUL TITEMPORAL CROP SEGMENTATION
CNN-based methodologies have prevailed in agricultural
segmentation jobs for numerous years, with U-Net laying
the groundwork for encoder-decoder architectures in remote
sensing applications [34], [35], [36]. DeepLabv3 incorporates
dilated convolutions to capture multi-scale information while
preserving spatial resolution, demonstrating efficacy in agri-
cultural land classification tasks [29], [37]. Feature Pyramid
Networks (FPN) improve multi-scale feature integration
via lateral connections, facilitating superior recognition of
crop fields across various scales [29], [38]. Nonetheless,
these CNN-based approaches encounter difficulties in tem-
poral sequence modeling due to their intrinsic constraint
in capturing long-range dependencies within time series
data [6], [15], [18]. The localized receptive fields of
convolutional procedures do not adequately capture global
temporal patterns necessary for differentiating crops with
similar spectral traits but varying phenological cycles [16],
[17], [39].
Transformer-based approaches have emerged as
formidable options for managing temporal dependencies
in agricultural segmentation, with Vision Transformers
(ViT) exhibiting exceptional efficacy in capturing global
linkages within image sequences [40], [41]. Swin-UNETR
integrates the hierarchical feature extraction abilities of the
Swin Transformer with the U-Net decoder architecture,
demonstrating favorable outcomes for multi-temporal crop
segmentation tasks [42]. The self-attention mechanism
in Transformers facilitates the successful modeling of
long-range temporal dependencies, enhancing the discrim-
ination of crops with analogous spectral signatures yet
differing temporal patterns [5], [29], [43]. The shifted
window methodology of the Swin Transformer decreases
computational complexity relative to conventional Vision
Transformers while preserving the ability to simulate global
context [41]. Notwithstanding these benefits, transformer-
based methodologies continue to have quadratic computing
difficulty concerning sequence length, thereby constraining
their scalability for extended temporal sequences commonly
found in agricultural monitoring applications [44], [45], [46].
Both the CNN and transformer methodologies encounter
considerable difficulties in processing prolonged temporal
sequences, which are essential for thorough agricultural
monitoring during growing seasons [6], [15], [16], [18].
Convolutional neural networks require very deep architec-
tures to obtain sufficient temporal receptive fields, resulting
in heightened processing expenses and possible gradient
vanishing issues [5], [47], [48]. Although adept at modeling
global temporal linkages, transformers become computa-
tionally unfeasible for lengthy sequences because of their
quadratic scaling in self-attention mechanisms [5], [44],
[45]. The sensitivity to temporal order in agricultural data,
characterized by specific sequences of phenological stages,
necessitates models capable of rapidly processing extended
temporal sequences while preserving spatial accuracy for
precise crop border delineation [30], [31], [49]. Current
methodologies often compromise between the effectiveness
of temporal modeling and computational efficiency, high-
lighting the need for alternative architectures that can achieve
both objectives simultaneously.
C. STATE-SPACE MODELS AND SEQUENTIAL PROCESSING
IN COMPUTER VISION APPLICATIONS
State-space models (SSMs) are developed from conventional
Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to overcome the chal-
lenges of vanishing gradients and suboptimal parallelization
in sequence modeling jobs [50], [51]. Recurrent Neural
Networks (RNNs), encompassing Long Short-Term Mem-
ory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) versions,
exhibited initial efficacy in temporal sequence processing;
nevertheless, they encountered computational limitations
stemming from their sequential architecture and gradient
propagation issues in extended sequences [10], [50], [51].
SSMs originated from control theory for modeling dynamic
systems and were subsequently adopted for machine learning
applications, providing enhanced capacity for managing
long-range dependencies with improved training stabil-
ity [52], [53]. The mathematical basis of SSMs facilitates
continuous-time modeling that may be discretized for prac-
tical application, offering superior theoretical justification
compared to heuristic RNN architectures [54]. Contemporary
SSMs integrate selective processes that enable adaptive focus
on relevant information while effectively managing lengthy
sequences, thereby addressing the inherent limitations of
earlier recurrent architectures [55].
The Mamba architecture signifies a substantial advance-
ment in SSM design, incorporating SSM methods that
provide effective processing of lengthy sequences with linear
computing complexity [10], [11]. The selective mechanism
of Mamba enables the model to actively filter and retain
pertinent information based on input content, resulting
in enhanced performance relative to conventional SSMs
with static parameters [10], [56]. The hardware-aware
implementation of Mamba improves GPU utilization by
employing parallel scanning methods and kernel fusion
techniques, which leads to significant efficiency gains in
practical applications [10], [57]. Vision Mamba (VMamba)
effectively modified the Mamba architecture for computer
vision applications by incorporating 2D Selective Scan mod-
ules that efficiently process spatial input while preserving
global receptive fields [13], [14]. The applications of Mamba
in computer vision have exhibited competitive efficacy
compared to Transformers while necessitating considerably
fewer computational resources, especially for high-resolution
pictures and video processing jobs [13], [14].
SegMamba specifically tackles 3D segmentation difficul-
ties by enhancing Mamba’s sequential processing capabilities
for volumetric data, incorporating the Tri-Oriented Mamba

D. WAVELET TRANSFORM APPLICATIONS IN REMOTE
SENSING ANAL YSIS
Multi-resolution analysis via wavelet transformation has
become a crucial method in remote sensing applications,
facilitating the concurrent examination of spatial character-
istics at several scales while maintaining both frequency and
spatial domain information [58], [59]. The Discrete Wavelet
Transform (DWT) disaggregates remote sensing imagery into
four sub-bands: one low-frequency approximation compo-
nent and three high-frequency detail components that depict
horizontal, vertical, and diagonal characteristics [59]. This
decomposition enables researchers to examine global trends
via low-frequency components while maintaining fine-scale
features in high-frequency sub-bands, which is especially
beneficial for detecting tiny variations in vegetation indices
and crop traits. The reversible characteristic of wavelet
transformation guarantees information retention without loss
during both decomposition and reconstruction, making it
appropriate for applications necessitating high-fidelity fea-
ture extraction [60]. Remote sensing applications leverage the
capacity of wavelets to manage diverse scales of agricultural
features, including individual crop rows, field boundaries,
and landscape patterns, within a cohesive mathematical
framework.
Wavelet-Vision Transformer (Wave-ViT) designs have
shown considerable advancements in computer vision tasks
by combining multi-resolution wavelet analysis with self-
attention mechanisms [61]. Wave-ViT executes lossless
down-sampling via a discrete wavelet transform (DWT),
thereby reducing the computational burden of self-attention
while maintaining spatial information that would otherwise
be lost in conventional pooling methods. The design employs
individual self-attention methods on wavelet sub-bands,
enabling the model to autonomously concentrate on distinct
frequency components before reconstruction via inverse
DWT [61]. This method facilitates the fast processing
of high-resolution images while preserving transformers’
global context modeling abilities, thus overcoming the
computational constraints that confine transformer appli-
cations to low-resolution inputs. The modular architecture
of Wave-ViT facilitates adaptable integration with diverse
backbone frameworks, showcasing versatility in numerous
computer vision applications, such as object identification
and semantic segmentation.
In remote sensing, wavelet transformation functions as an
effective feature improvement method by augmenting signal-
to-noise ratios and emphasizing pertinent spectral-spatial
patterns while diminishing extraneous variations [60], [62].
The multiscale decomposition capability facilitates the tar-
geted augmentation of features at particular scales, thereby
improving the differentiation of crop varieties that possess
analogous spectral characteristics yet exhibit distinct spatial
textures or patterns [62]. Wavelet-based denoising methods
efficiently eliminate air disturbances and sensor noise from
satellite images while maintaining essential edge informa-
tion vital for precise boundary delineation in agricultural
segmentation applications [63]. The frequency separation
characteristics of wavelets facilitate the precise processing
of various scale components, enabling the improvement
of fine-scale details, such as crop row patterns, while
preserving information about large-scale field structures. The
combination of wavelet transformation and deep learning
architectures has demonstrated encouraging outcomes in
enhancing feature representation quality, especially for
applications necessitating the analysis of multi-scale spatial
patterns commonly found in agricultural remote sensing
data [64].
III. PROPOSED METHOD
A. OVERALL ARCHITECTURE
GWSC-SegMamba introduces an innovative hybrid archi-
tecture that combines state-space models with wavelet
transformation and spatial convolution elements to address
multi-temporal agricultural segmentation issues [10], [14],
[47], [59]. The framework integrates the linear com-
puting efficiency of the Mamba architecture with the
multi-resolution analytical features of wavelet transformation
and spatial dependency modeling via gated convolution meth-
ods [53], [61], [65], [66]. Our design handles multi-temporal
satellite images with dimensions C× T× H× W , where
C signifies spectral channels, T indicates temporal depth,
and H× W delineates spatial dimensions [6], [18]. The
primary novelty is the seamless integration of GWC and
GSC components within the TSWMamba blocks, facilitating
concurrent multiscale feature extraction and spatial context
modeling [36], [38], [67]. This hybrid method overcomes
the shortcomings of current techniques by enabling efficient
temporal sequence processing while preserving high spatial
resolution and quality of feature representation [34], [35].
The encoder-decoder architecture employs a hierarchical
framework tailored for multi-temporal data processing, where
the encoder systematically diminishes spatial dimensions
while augmenting channel depth to encapsulate multi-scale

B. TSWMAMBA BLOCK ENHANCEMENT
The TSWMamba block signifies a vital modification of the
original 3D SegMamba architecture, designed to effectively
process multi-temporal 2D satellite imagery, hence shifting
the processing framework from volumetric medical data to
temporal remote sensing sequences [6], [10], [12], [15], [18].
We altered the original 3D convolution processes to explicitly
handle temporal dimensions, transforming the input format
from C× D× H× W (volumetric) to C× T× H× W (multi-
temporal) to align with the satellite time series data [68].
The adaptation entails reconfiguring the depth dimension
processing into temporal sequence modeling, with each time
step denoting a satellite observation instead of a spatial slice
in volumetric data [50]. This change allows the model to
utilize temporal patterns unique to agricultural phenology,
including crop growth cycles and seasonal variations, which
fundamentally differ from the spatial correlations found in
medical volumetric data [16], [17], [19]. The TSWMamba
block preserves the original architecture’s computational
efficiency while modifying the processing flow to capture the

VII. CONCLUSION
This study presents GWSC-SegMamba, an innovative hybrid
architecture that combines state space models with GWC
and GSC components for multi-temporal agricultural field
segmentation. Extensive experimental assessment across
three benchmark datasets indicates that GWSC-SegMamba
achieves notable enhancements in segmentation accuracy
while preserving linear computational complexity. The
architecture achieved a 7.65% increase in mean IoU relative
to conventional SegMamba and a 4.43% improvement over
the Swin-UNETR baseline, with significant advancements of
53.13% in IoU for difficult minority classes, such as winter
triticale. Statistical significance assessment with bootstrap-
based t-tests validated the consistency of these enhancements,
with effect sizes varying from large to very large across
several evaluation measures.
The component-wise analysis indicated that the Gate
Wavelet Convolution component significantly contributes
to the overall accuracy metrics, whereas the Gate Spa-
tial Convolution component improves the spatial context
modeling and border delineation. The wavelet component’s
multi-resolution analysis capabilities efficiently mitigate the
spatial resolution constraints of medium-resolution satellite
data, facilitating enhanced differentiation across spectrally
analogous crop types. The analysis revealed dataset-specific
performance trends, with architectural modifications yielding
significant advantages on the Munich and PASTIS datasets
but demonstrating minimal improvements on the Lombardia
datasets. The per-class study revealed significant failure
modes for specific minority classes, suggesting optimization
issues necessitating additional examination.
The computational complexity analysis reveals that
GWSC-SegMamba exhibits competitive efficiency with an
approximate 8-10% computational overhead relative to the
baseline SegMamba, while offering markedly superior scal-
ing characteristics compared to transformer-based method-
ologies. The linear complexity scaling with input dimensions
and embedding sizes renders the architecture especially
apt for processing high-resolution multi-temporal satellite
imagery in operational agricultural monitoring systems. The
qualitative assessment corroborates the quantitative results,
demonstrating enhanced border preservation and diminished
classification mistakes, particularly in intricate agricultural
scenarios necessitating detailed temporal pattern recognition.
Future studies should focus on employing adaptive tech-
niques that more effectively manage class imbalance to
overcome the architectural constraints identified in minority
class segmentation. Advancements in automated hyperpa-
rameter optimization techniques may enhance the resilience
of the model across various agricultural scenarios and
datasets. Moreover, expanding the validation to encompass
multi-country and multi-climate datasets will enhance the
claims of generalizability and enable wider operational
implementation. The integration of multisensor data sources,
such as SAR and hyperspectral photography, could signifi-
cantly improve the discriminative capacities for difficult crop
varieties. Real-time processing capabilities and edge deploy-
ment optimization would facilitate practical application in
