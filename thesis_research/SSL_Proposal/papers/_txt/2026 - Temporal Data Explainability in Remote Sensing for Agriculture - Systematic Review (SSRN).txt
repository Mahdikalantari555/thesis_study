Temporal Data Explainability in Remote Sensing for Agriculture:
A Systematic Review
Boyuan Zheng , Bin Liang , Shuming Liang , Fang Chen
Data Science Institute, University of Technology Sydney, 15 Broadway, Ultimo, Sydney, 2007, NSW, Australia
Abstract
Remote sensing is playing an increasingly important role in agriculture, enhancing capabilities in monitoring, manage-
ment, and planning. With the growing availability of data resources such as satellites and drones, there is a corresponding
demand for advanced data processing, modeling techniques, and prediction explanations. This survey examines the in-
tersection of temporal explainability, remote sensing, and agriculture through a Systematic Literature Review (SLR).
We analyze the utilization of temporal data, the methods employed to uncover temporal patterns, and the types of
information explained using Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) in agricultural applications. The findings highlight
a diverse spectrum of preprocessing techniques, machine learning models, and temporal analysis strategies tailored to
agricultural needs, with a growing emphasis on stage-based temporal modeling and stakeholder-centered explainability.
Despite significant advancements, challenges remain in aggregating temporal features, adopting advanced sequential ar-
chitectures, and evaluating explanation quality. Addressing these challenges is essential for building trust and enabling
practical, data-driven solutions in agriculture. Future research should prioritize the development of specialized remote
sensing techniques and model architectures that align more closely with agricultural practices, incorporating domain
expertise and stakeholder feedback to enhance transparency and trust in AI-driven agriculture.
Keywords: Explainable artificial intelligence, Temporal explainability, Remote sensing, Agriculture

4.3. What kinds of information are explained using XAI?499
To investigate RQ 3, we filtered the literature to focus500

Broadly, XAI methods applied in agriculture fall into510

Ensemble explanation refers to explainability techniques535

In these models, predictions are generated by aggregat-538

The significance of ensemble explanation in the context of544

portant in precision agriculture applications. One of the675

satility makes perturbation-based explanations particu-681
larly valuable for agricultural and environmental mod-682
eling, where heterogeneous data sources—including soil683

et al., 2025), remote sensing indices (Xue et al., 2024),685

niques, which segment temporal sequences into overlap-690
ping windows and calculate time-window-specific feature691
importance. This approach not only quantifies the evolv-692
ing influence of features over time, but also helps to cap-693
ture temporal dependencies, which are critical for under-694
standing seasonal crop development, weather impacts, and695
dynamic soil-plant interactions. This combined spatial-696
temporal mapping capability makes perturbation-based697
explanations highly compatible with precision agriculture698
workflows, where understanding within-field and within-699
season variability is essential for site-specific and adaptive700
management. Combined with the increasing availability701

to agronomically interpretable factors, ensuring that ad-705
vanced machine learning models remain practically action-706
able for growers, agronomists, and land managers.707

5.1. Data Representation749
Referring to Victor et al. (2022), remote sensing data750
could be represented as spectral sequence, spectral im-751
age cube, object-based feature stack and so on (see Fig.752
5). Object-based representation is fundamental for inter-753
preting remote sensing data in regional agriculture, es-754
pecially in contexts like orchards, where detailed anal-755
ysis enhances precision farming practices. In practical756
applications, many research related to yield prediction,757
management optimization are tend to represent their in-758
put as object-based. Aggregating climate variables with759
object-based representatiion over large agricultural regions760
presents challenges in downscaling and maintaining spatial761
resolution (Cai et al., 2019). Accurate downsampling is762
essential to ensure that aggregated climate data provides763
field-level insights while retaining regional relevance. For764
instance, assessing temperature or precipitation patterns765
across vast areas requires temporal consistency to detect766
trends crucial for crop health and resilience. Effective ag-767
gregation methods must address spatial heterogeneity and768
temporal alignment to produce reliable climate-informed769
predictions, ensuring that XAI delivers trustworthy in-770
sights for both localized and regional applications. Future771
work could explore advanced downscaling and alignment772
techniques that leverage multi-resolution fusion, enabling773
climate-informed predictions with high spatial precision774
for enhanced decision-making across different agricultural775
scales (Wang et al., 2024).776
Temporal feature aggregation is instrumental in captur-777
ing and simplifying patterns over specific time intervals,778
such as seasonal shifts or crop cycle stages. In agriculture,779
aggregating data by growth stages, climate cycles, or en-780
vironmental phases can reduce noise and clarify detected781
patterns. This approach enhances explainable AI applica-782
tions by providing stakeholders with contextually relevant783
temporal summaries, aiding in understanding how sea-784
sonal variations impact crop growth and yield (Hrast Es-785
senfelder et al., 2025). However, achieving effective aggre-786
gation requires careful selection of timeframes and granu-787
larity levels to retain critical information, striking a bal-788
ance between model complexity and interpretability. To789
further this area, future studies should investigate adap-790
tive temporal aggregation methods that dynamically ad-791
just to crop cycles and environmental changes, aiming to792
capture nuanced seasonal shifts and other critical patterns793
in agricultural datasets.794

niques to address issues such as atmospheric distortions806
and geolocation mismatches (Gilbertson and Van Niekerk,807
2017). Developing standardized preprocessing pipelines808
would improve data consistency across modalities, enhanc-809
ing the reliability and interpretability of AI models in810
agriculture. A promising direction would be the devel-811
opment of unified preprocessing frameworks or protocols812
that standardize corrections across data sources, facilitat-813
ing greater consistency and reliability in multi-modal agri-814
cultural analyses.815
The literature on agricultural remote sensing heavily816

sion models, which could enhance temporal pattern de-819
tection. Much of the current AI research in agricul-820
