ABSTRACT
Accurate crop mapping plays a critical role in optimizing agricultural monitoring and ensuring food security. 
Although data-driven deep learning methods have demonstrated success in crop mapping with satellite image 
time series (SITS) data, their promising performances heavily depend on labeled training samples. Nevertheless, 
the difficulty of annotating crop types often results in labeled data scarcity, leading to a decline in the model’s 
performance. Self-supervised learning (SSL) is a novel technique for crop mapping with limited labels. However, 
the existing SSL methods applied to SITS data typically explore masking solely on temporal dimension, which 
cannot guarantee strong spatial representation and therefore hinders the accurate prediction of complex crop 
fields. Furthermore, these methods sequentially extract spatial and temporal information without fully inte-
grating information across different dimensions. In this study, we propose a spatiotemporal masking strategy for 
pre-training a SpatioTemporal Collaborative Learning Network (STCLN) to extract informative spatial and 
temporal representations from SITS data. Additionally, we design a SpatioTemporal Attention (STA) module in 
STCLN that integrates representations from spatial and temporal dimensions. The experimental results on two 
crop type mapping benchmarks encompassing various crop types demonstrate the outperformance of our pro-
posed method. STCLN_wp outperforms the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods with 6.49% higher mIoU on 
PASTIS dataset and 4.04% higher mIoU on MTLCC dataset. The ablation experiments on pre-training, masking 
strategies, and the STA module validate the effectiveness of our methodological design. Additionally, experi-
ments conducted under varying sizes of the training set highlight the superior generalization ability of our 
method for crop type mapping in label-scarce situations. The code of our method is available at https://github.co 
m/XiaoleiQinn/STCLN.
1. Introduction
Achieving sustainable global food security is challenging, especially 
as the United Nations (UN) projects the global population to reach 9.7 
billion by 2050 (UN, 2022). Inequitable access to nutritious food and 
climate change further complicate agricultural management (Cole et al., 
2018). Given these challenges, accurate crop monitoring, including 
mapping crop distribution, is essential for effective agricultural man-
agement and informed food policy (Konduri et al., 2020). The high 
temporal and spatial resolution of remote sensing imagery has facili-
tated the dynamic monitoring of land cover through multi-temporal 
views derived from satellite image time series (SITS) data (Xu et al., 
2021). Information extracted from SITS data offers valuable insights into 
the temporal patterns of crop growth, allowing for more comprehensive 
and informed decision-making in agriculture and land management.
Recent studies have shown that data-driven deep learning (DL) 
methods excel at recognizing crucial sequential dependencies from SITS 
(Li and Wu, 2024). There are mainly four strategies for SITS processing: 
Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), Convolutional Neural Network 
(CNN), Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), and hybrid models. RNN 
leverages recursive connections to capture temporal correlations, 
enabling information to propagate from one sequence step to the next. 
Due to its capability of discerning patterns and dependencies over suc-
cessive temporal observations, RNN-based methods have been widely 
adopted for identifying crop types using time series data (Ienco et al., 
2017; Rußwurm and K¨orner, 2017; Yuan et al., 2020). CNN-based

3D-CNN for spatial–temporal features (Rustowicz et al., 2019). On the 
other hand, the Transformer processes all temporal positions simulta-
neously and models the interplay between each position through the 
calculation of attention scores. It has shown superior performance in 
crop mapping (Rußwurm and K¨orner, 2020; Tarasiou et al., 2023). 
Unlike the aforementioned methods that rely on a single type of archi-
tecture for spatial and temporal representation, hybrid architectures 
such as CNN-RNN (Benedetti et al., 2018; Chamorro Martinez et al., 
2021; Li et al., 2020; Qu et al., 2024; Rußwurm and K¨orner, 2018; 
Rustowicz et al., 2019) and CNN-Transformer (Garnot and Landrieu, 
2021; Hu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2020) have been developed to combine 
the strengths of different architectures and integrate multi-dimensional 
features for crop mapping.
While supervised deep learning approaches have demonstrated 
effectiveness, their promising performance often hinges on the avail-
ability of a large number of training samples. The accuracy and 
robustness of the model tend to increase with a greater number of 
samples. However, the process of collecting ground truth data is both 
time-consuming and laborious (Nguyen et al., 2022), and the avail-
ability of crop type labels is often limited in practical applications (You 
et al., 2023). The scarcity of training samples poses a challenge in crop 
mapping, potentially leading to overfitting issues and constraining the 
performance of supervised deep learning methods in this domain (Sun 
et al., 2024; Xue et al., 2022).
Compared to the difficulty of obtaining labeled data, it is much easier 
to acquire a large amount of unlabeled remote sensing images. The 
unlabeled data becomes more accessible with the advent of satellite 
missions like Sentinel-2, which have been widely adopted in crop 
mapping tasks due to their high spatial resolution and frequent revisit 
intervals. These unlabeled remote sensing images provides a valuable 
resource for self-supervised learning (SSL) approaches, allowing models 
to learn meaningful representations without the need for explicit la-
beling (Wang et al., 2023). In contrast to fully supervised learning 
methods that rely on labeled data, SSL approaches utilize the inherent 
structure of the data for supervision, enabling neural networks to 
autonomously extract versatile features from unlabeled data (He et al., 
2020; Misra and Maaten, 2020). The SSL pipeline involves pre-training a 
network on unlabeled data using proxy tasks to learn high-level features, 
then fine-tuning it on a labeled dataset for a specific task (Kolesnikov 
et al., 2019). Recent self-supervised approaches for images can be 
mainly categorized as contrastive or generative (Hong et al., 2024). 
Contrastive methods learn representations by reducing the distance 
between paired samples in the embedded space (Xu et al., 2024), while 
generative methods aim to learn representations by reconstructing the 
input data from a compressed latent space (Yuan and Lin, 2021). Thanks 
to the popularity of SSL methods in the computer vision domain, this 
technique is receiving increased attention in remote sensing and SITS 
processing.
To incorporate SSL technique into SITS processing, various pre- 
training methods based on temporal masking and reconstruction 
(Tseng et al., 2023; Yuan and Lin, 2021) as well as contrastive learning 
(Xu et al., 2024) have been proposed. However, these methods are pixel- 
based and do not consider spatial information, limiting their ability to 
capture the complex spatial patterns inherent in agricultural parcels. To 
exploit spatial information, patch-based (Yuan et al., 2022) and image- 
based architectures (Dumeur et al., 2024) for spatiotemporal encoding 
have been developed. These methods randomly select image patches 
along the temporal dimension for masking, which mainly strengthens 
the temporal encoder’s representation capability during pre-training. 
However, there are still some challenges that need to be addressed: 
(1) Regarding the pre-training strategy, the previous methods mask

To address these issues, we design a spatiotemporal masked pre- 
training task incorporating a SpatioTemporal Collaborative Learning 
Network (STCLN) to improve crop mapping under limited sample con-
ditions. Unlike the temporal masking mechanism in previous work, our 
spatiotemporal masking strategy improves the model’s capacity to 
concurrently learn both spatial and temporal features during pre- 
training. Moreover, we introduce a SpatioTemporal Attention (STA) 
module during fine-tuning, which explicitly correlates spatial and tem-
poral encoders to integrate latent representations across dimensions. 
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: 
1. We propose a spatiotemporal masking strategy for pre-training the 
STCLN. This spatiotemporal masking strategy enables STCLN to 
learn enhanced spatial and temporal representations during pre- 
training, thereby addressing the challenge of insufficient spatial 
representation in complex crop fields.
2. We propose a STA module to establish explicit correlations between 
the spatial and temporal encoders, thereby enhancing feature rep-
resentation across different dimensions.
3. Extensive experimental results on regions with complex crop fields 
and limited labeled samples demonstrate the effectiveness of the 
proposed spatiotemporal masking and STCLN framework.

study areas and datasets. Section 3 introduces the proposed method. The 
details of the experimental results are reported in Section 4. Section 5
discusses the robustness and limitations of our work. Finally, Section 6
concludes this paper.
2. Study areas and data
The experiments are conducted on two datasets that represent 
complex crop dynamics and various crop types. Fig. 1 shows study areas 
of Panoptic Agricultural Satellite TIme-Series (PASTIS) datasets (Garnot 
and Landrieu, 2021). This dataset covers four Sentinel tile regions in 
France, covering approximately 4000 km2. Images in the PASTIS dataset 
have been preprocessed through atmospheric correction, cloud removal, 
and resampling to a spatial resolution of 10 m per pixel. The training and 
testing data are selected from separate folds of the dataset, with a 1 km 
buffer between images to prevent cross-fold contamination. Each patch 
contains a sequence of multi-spectral satellite images with 10 bands and 
a resolution of 128 × 128 pixels. The images are acquired by the 
Sentinel-2 satellites from September 2018 to November 2019, with the 
number of observations at each location ranging from 38 to 61. With the 
extent and content information of all parcels retrieved from French Land 
Parcel Identification System (FLPIS), each pixel in the PASTIS dataset is 
annotated with semantic label. Excluding the background and void 
label, the prediction task involves a total of 18 classes. Considering the 
memory usage limitation during training, the images were cropped to a 
size of 32 × 32. We use 7936 images for pre-training. Our training 
dataset consisted of 152 image time series, with another 152 used for 
validation and 7712 for testing. This training dataset size constitutes 
roughly 2 % of the samples from one fold of the original PASTIS dataset.
The second dataset we adopted is the Multi-Temporal Land Cover 
Classification (MTLCC) dataset, as shown in Fig. 2. It is constructed from 
a region of 102 km × 42 km located in Munich, Germany (Rußwurm and 
K¨orner, 2018). This dataset contains Sentinel-2 products gathered from 
2016, which are constructed into 6248 patches with image size of 48 ×
48, feature depth of 13, and the temporal length of 30. We adopt the 
official training set of the MTLCC dataset without labels for self-

3. Method
3.1. Overview
The workflow of our method is shown in Fig. 3. We introduce a self- 
supervised pre-training task that first masks partial pixels of unlabeled 
SITS and then reconstructs them. This stage aims to facilitate the model 
in autonomously learning spatiotemporal representations directly from 
the data. Upon convergence, the model weights from the pre-trained 
network are used to initialize the crop type prediction network. Given 
the initialization from pre-training, the network is fine-tuned, utilizing a 
limited set of labeled data for crop type prediction. After the network is 
well-trained, it can be deployed to map crop types in the remaining 
areas.
3.2. Spatiotemporal collaborative learning network (STCLN)
We design a Spatiotemporal Collaborative Learning Network 
(STCLN) for crop type classification using SITS data. As shown in Fig. 4, 
STCLN contains the SEncoder, TEncoder, and STA modules. In this 
section, we introduce our proposed STCLN and its components in detail.
3.2.1. Spatial encoder (SEncoder)
The SEncoder concurrently and independently processes each image 
in the sequence, and its structure is shown in Fig. 5. It comprises two 
DoubleConv layers (Li et al., 2022). Each DoubleConv layer consists of 
two 3 × 3 convolutions with a padding of 1. Every convolution is fol-
lowed by batch normalization (BN) and a rectified linear unit (ReLU). 
The number of filters in these two convolutional layers is {32, 256}, 
respectively. For an input sequence of SITS data, S∈ RB×T×C×H×W (with 
B as the batch size, T as the temporal length, C as the number of chan-
nels, H as the image height, and W as the image width), the temporal and 
batch dimensions are flattened and the data is reshaped to Sʹ∈
R(B×T)×C×H×W before being processed by SEncoder. SEncoder focuses 
solely on learning the spatial representation of the SITS by applying 
Conv2D to each temporal position. With convolutions retaining spatial 
resolution via padding of 1 and a kernel size of 3 × 3, SEncoder produces 
a feature map with dimensions of (B× T)× Cʹ× H× W, where Cʹ is set to 
256 in the last convolution calculation. The output feature map of 
SEncoder is reshaped to (B× H× W)× T× Cʹ, providing an adaptive 
input for the temporal encoder (TEncoder).

3.3. Self-supervised pre-training
As described above, the STCLN comprises the SEncoder, TEncoder, 
and STA modules. SEncoder and TEncoder successively process tempo-
ral and spatial information, and they are first pre-trained to learn the 
spatiotemporal representations from the abundant unlabeled SITS data. 
Fig. 7 illustrates the pipeline of the proposed pre-training and fine- 
tuning stages.
To extract informative features from unlabeled data without relying 
on human annotation, we design a novel proxy task that trains the 
network to reconstruct the missing regions of input image time series. 
Specifically, we assume that the input image time series are 
S∈ RT×C×H×W, with T being the length of the sequence, C the number of 
channels, H the image height, and W the image width. A certain pro-
portion of pixels in S are randomly replaced with a learnable mask value 
before being fed into the network, and we refer to the partially masked 
SITS as Sm. Although the temporal masking strategy has shown prom-
ising results on time series of image patches in previous studies (Dumeur 
et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2022), there remains uncertainty about its 
effectiveness in facilitating the model to learn spatial representation 
when applied to the entire image (shown in Fig. 8 (a)). To facilitate 
spatial feature learning, we designed the spatiotemporal masking 
strategy (as shown in Fig. 8 (b)), which demonstrates better performance

3.4. Supervised fine-tuning
Once the model converges in the pre-training stage, the weights of 
SEncoder and TEncoder are utilized to initialize the corresponding 
components of STCLN in the fine-tuning stage. Fine-tuning, a technique 
commonly used in transfer learning methods, was introduced as a 
method to adapt a pre-trained model to a specific task or dataset 
(Yosinski et al., 2014). It involves utilizing samples from the target 
domain to adjust either the entire pre-trained model or specific parts of 
it, enabling the model to adapt to a new task or dataset (Wang et al., 
2022b). In this study, we have incorporated the fine-tuning technique to 
adjust the parameters of our pre-trained model, tailoring it specifically 
to the target task. The pre-trained weights of the SEncoder and TEncoder 
are loaded during the fine-tuning process. Following TEncoder, the STA 
module is introduced to enrich the spatiotemporal representation by 
processing the feature maps derived from both the SEncoder and TEn-
coder. Following the encoding process, the Decoder, which comprises a 
linear mapping layer, is employed to predict the probability for each 
category, ultimately resulting in a crop classification map. Specifically, 
the input dimension of the Decoder is 256, which is the same as the 
output dimension of TEncoder. The output dimension of the Decoder is 
the same as the number of classes.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental setting
In this section, we conduct comparative experiments with SOTA 
methods in crop type mapping. We consider a diverse set of methods 
encompassing convolutional, recurrent, and attention-based architec-
tures for temporal feature extraction. Spatial representation learning 
architectures in our comparison include UNet, Dense Fully Convolu-
tional Network (FCN), and Atrous Convolution. Eight SOTA methods, 
namely 3D U-Net (Rustowicz et al., 2019), BCLSTM (Rußwurm and 
K¨orner, 2018), UTAE (Garnot and Landrieu, 2021), BUnetConvLSTM 
(Chamorro Martinez et al., 2021), BAtrousConvLSTM (Chamorro Mar-
tinez et al., 2021), U-BARN (Dumeur et al., 2024), SITS-Former (Yuan 
et al., 2022), and SITS-MoCo (Xu et al., 2024) are selected for a 
comprehensive comparison of crop classification results. To elaborate on 
the architectures, 3D U-Net, UTAE, BUnetConvLSTM adopt a U-shaped 
network structure with both downsampling and upsampling paths for 
image semantic segmentation. While 3D U-Net simultaneously learns 
spatiotemporal information through 3D convolutional computation, 
UTAE and BUnetConvLSTM encode temporal information using tem-
poral attention and Bi-directional ConvLSTM, respectively. BCLSTM 
employs convolutional recurrent layers that consider both spatial and 
temporal context, distinguishing it from traditional LSTM. The 
BAtrousConvLSTM model combines the characteristics of the 
BConvLSTM network with the inherent capability of ASPP (Atrous 
Spatial Pyramid Pooling) to extract features at multiple scales. Novel 
and classic self-supervised methods for SITS, such as U-BARN, SITS- 
Former, and SITS-MoCo, are chosen for comparison. These methods 
utilize the Transformer for extracting temporal features within SITS and 
consist of both pre-training and fine-tuning stages. For a fair compari-
son, they are pre-trained and fine-tuned on the same dataset as our 
proposed method. Specifically, the input dataset is arranged in chro-
nological order based on the dates of the data collection. Our model with 
pre-training is denoted as STCLN_wp, while the model without pre- 
training is named STCLN.
4.2. Implementation details
We pre-train our model for 100 epochs with a batch size of 4 and a 
learning rate of 0.0001, followed by fine-tuning on labeled training data 
for an additional 100 epochs. The networks are trained using PyTorch on 
4 RTX 3090 GPUs. For the masking implementation, we randomly mask 
40 % of the pixels in the SITS data. Specifically, the mask value is a 
learnable parameter optimized by the training process, similar to the 
popular research (Tong et al., 2022). Recognizing that cloud-covered 
areas are considered noise and do not contribute meaningfully to the 
reconstruction process, we exclude potential cloud regions from the 
masked positions to enhance the quality of temporal feature capture and 
reconstruction. For optimization, we employ the Adam optimizer 
(Kingma and Ba, 2014) during both pre-training and fine-tuning stages. 
We apply the same pre-training and fine-tuning settings for U-BARN, 
SITS-Former, and SITS-MoCo. For 3D U-Net, BCLSTM, UTAE, BUnet-
ConvLSTM, BAtrousConvLSTM, we train them from scratch using the 
same parameters as our fine-tuning process.
Three evaluation metrics, including overall accuracy (OA), inter-
section over union (IoU), and F1 score (Dumeur et al., 2024), are used to 
assess the performance of the proposed crop type mapping method. To 
comprehensively assess the performance of the methods, we also 
consider the average IoU (mIoU) and average F1 score (mF1).

To assess the effectiveness of the proposed STA module and pre-

Comparing Ablation 1 and Ablation 3, it can be observed that the

Ablation 3 with Ablation 6. Overall, the combined addition of the STA 
module and pre-training step demonstrates a significant performance 
boost, yielding improvements of 10.2 % in mIoU (comparing Ablation 1 
and 6).

5. Discussion
5.1. Analysis of robustness
We evaluate the performance of the methods with different numbers 
of labeled samples. Each model is trained or fine-tuned using a variable 
number of training samples ranging from 1 % to 5 % of the original 
training sample size. The number of validation samples is set to be the 
same as the training set, while the testing set remains unchanged across 
all settings.
As shown in Fig. 13, we can observe a consistent improvement in 
accuracy across all methods as the sample size increases, highlighting a 
positive correlation between the training sample size and model’s 
effectiveness. Notably, methods utilizing self-attention for temporal 
encoding (i.e., STCLN_wp, STCLN, SITS-Former, SITS-MoCo, U-BARN, 
and U-TAE) outperform other approaches across different training 
sample sizes. Among the SSL methods, the accuracies of U-BARN and 
SITS-MoCo are lower than those of the other approaches. Additionally, 
STCLN_wp demonstrates superior performance compared to STCLN. 
With a 1 % proportion of the training set, STCLN_wp shows a perfor-
mance improvement of 1.0–4.2 % in OA and 7.5–17.1 % in mIoU over 
STCLN in the two datasets. This underscores the efficacy of pre-training 
in enhancing the model’s performance under conditions with extremely 
limited samples. As the training sample size increases to 5 % of the 
original training set, the accuracy gain from pre-training diminishes to 
4.9–5.5 % in mIoU. Overall, this result demonstrates that STCLN_wp can 
enhance crop mapping even with varying amounts of limited labeled 
samples.
Compared to MTLCC dataset, methods perform worse on the PASTIS 
dataset, possibly due to its larger spatial extent and 1-km buffer zone

5.2. Advantages and limitations
We propose a spatiotemporal masking strategy and a STA module to 
enhance the model’s capability. Spatiotemporal masking outperforms 
temporal masking by forcing the model to learn more robust and 
detailed spatial representations during pre-training, which is crucial for 
crop type mapping tasks involving complex spatial patterns. The STA 
module explicitly correlates the spatial and temporal encoders via a 
multi-dimensional attention mechanism. This integration allows the 
model to capture interactions between spatial and temporal features, 
resulting in improved crop type prediction performance. In addition to 
the experiments described, we explore applying our model to early- 
season crop mapping and cross-year transfer tasks. Further details are 
available on our code website.
Although the proposed STCLN_wp shows great performance in crop 
type classification, significant misclassifications still exist in certain 
categories. From Table 5 and Table 7, it is found that IoUs on some crop 
types are relatively low, such as spring barley, winter triticale, and 
mixed cereal in the PASTIS dataset, as well as winter spelt, winter trit-
icale, peas, and winter rye in the MTLCC dataset. The possible reason for 
such misclassification is the spectral or phenological similarity between 
crop types (Rußwurm and K¨orner, 2018). To cope with this problem, we 
will explore incorporating the spectral masking mechanism (Hong et al., 
2024) that masks pixels among all the spatial, spectral, and temporal 
dimensions. The masked values are predicted to force the model to learn 
not only spatiotemporal information but also channel correlations 
within SITS.
Moreover, the aforementioned SSL methods for SITS, including ours, 
are pre-trained using regional data, which may lack generalization when 
applied to other areas with more complex crop types. Increasing the 
model’s size and using larger-scale pre-training data may solve this 
problem and further reduce the need for labeled training samples (Wang 
et al., 2022a). Therefore, we will scale up the model and pre-train it on 
more diverse unlabeled SITS data.
Furthermore, our proposed STA module enhances model perfor-
mance but introduces additional parameters that may complicate the 
model’s optimization, especially with limited computational resources. 
To address this issue, we plan to integrate the STA mechanism into pre- 
training, providing better initialization and reducing the optimization 
burden during fine-tuning.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce a spatiotemporal masked pre-training 
strategy and a SpatioTemporal Collaborative Learning Network 
(STCLN) for crop type mapping with SITS data. The spatiotemporal 
masking encourages the model to learn valuable spatiotemporal repre-
sentations through pre-training. To enhance the spatiotemporal repre-
sentation during fine-tuning, we propose the STA module that interacts 
information between spatial and temporal dimensions. Experimental 
results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves superior perfor-
mance. Ablation studies demonstrate that the integration of spatiotem-
poral masked pre-training and the STA module enhances the 
performance of STCLN, resulting in a 10.2 % improvement in mIoU. 
Furthermore, experiments on a variety of labeled sample sizes under-
score the outperformance of the proposed method under different levels 
of data scarcity. We will continue to refine the model to enhance its 
efficiency and broaden its application potential.
