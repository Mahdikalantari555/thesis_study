Hyperspectral Anomaly Change Detection
Xing Hu , Xiangcheng Liu, Chenxi Guo, Jiaxin Li , Member , IEEE, Qianqian Duan ,
Ruijiao Li , Senior Member , IEEE, Jing Cheng , and Dawei Zhang , Senior Member , IEEE
Abstract—Hyperspectral image change detection (CD) has gar-
nered signiﬁcant attention in the ﬁeld of remote sensing. A critical
task within CD is anomaly CD. Current generative anomaly CD
methods, such as diffusion models, typically rely on computation-
ally expensive iterative sampling to extract features, severely lim-
iting their real-time application capabilities. Furthermore, in the
absence of labeled data, existing unsupervised algorithms struggle
to effectively distinguish subtle target variations from background
artifacts caused by shadows or registration errors. To address these
challenges, we propose a novel hyperspectral anomaly CD method
named efﬁcient latent denoising-inspired network (ELDI-Net). It
employs a one-step manifold projection paradigm, achieving high
computational efﬁciency while preserving the noise-robustness ad-
vantages of generative models. Speciﬁcally, we introduce a one-
step latent manifold projection framework that transforms tra-
ditional iterative denoising into a deterministic latent mapping
via an encoder–projector–decoder architecture, achieving a sub-
stantial improvement in inference speed. In addition, a spectral
adaptive calibration projection module is constructed, employ-
ing channel-adaptive calibration to suppress spectral redundancy
while effectively preserving critical features of subtle targets. A
bidirectional focus alignment mechanism is designed for implicit

I. I NTRODUCTION
C
HANGE detection (CD) technology is a crucial technique
for monitoring modern urban development planning and
detecting land cover/land use changes [1], [2], [3], [4], [5],
[6], [7]. Generally, hyperspectral CD refers to identifying alter-
ations between two remote sensing images acquired at the same
location but at different times [8], [9]. Hyperspectral images
(HSIs), owing to their rich and continuous spectral information,
possess unique advantages in remote sensing CD and serve as a
vital tool for monitoring changes in land cover conditions [10],
[11]. Hyperspectral CD aids in identifying subtle alterations,
with hyperspectral anomaly change detection (HACD) being a
key task. Unlike conventional CD [12], [13],H A C D[14], [15]
focuses on alterations caused by the displacement, emergence,
disappearance, or concealment of small-scale and rare objects.
Consequently, minor targets are easily lost during processing,
making it challenging to distinguish genuine changes from
background artifacts. Furthermore, existing generative mod-
els face signiﬁcant computational bottlenecks, compromising
real-time performance. Simultaneously, HACD is motivated by
highlighting these rare alterations, offering irreplaceable value in
scenarios, such as air defense early warning, disaster emergency
response, and military reconnaissance.
In recent years, deep learning has demonstrated tremendous
potential in remote sensing image processing [16], [17], par-
ticularly for CD tasks. Mou et al. [18] utilized convolutional
neural networks to extract spectral–spatial features from images
and combined them with recurrent neural networks to analyze
temporal dependencies between images. However, existing deep
learning approaches are predominantly data-driven, typically re-
quiring large amounts of labeled samples to train network param-
eters. Although abundant hyperspectral data are now accessible,

only a limited number of annotated datasets exist. This scarcity
primarily stems from the fact that preprocessing and annotation
of multitemporal HSI data demand specialized interpretation
skills and are highly time-consuming. Consequently, employing
unsupervised or semisupervised learning methods to address this
challenge has become an increasingly urgent need.
Currently, unsupervised deep learning methods [19] can be
primarily categorized into two types. The ﬁrst type employs the
network as a feature extractor, transforming raw images into a
new feature space where variations are more readily highlighted.
Training then proceeds by analyzing the difference maps to
identify altered and unchanged regions. For instance, SCCN
utilizes a denoising autoencoder to initialize twin networks,
employs a thresholding method to locate unchanged areas, and
optimizes model parameters by minimizing feature differences
among invariant pixels. Zhang et al. [20] adopted a similar ar-
chitecture and employed clustering to analyze feature difference
maps for CD. However, such methods often struggle to preserve
features of small targets during global feature compression or
dimensionality reduction. Since anomalies typically manifest as
objects with extremely low pixel coverage, such as vehicles,
their faint spectral signals are easily smoothed or ﬁltered out as
noise, leading to high false-negative rates. The second category
involves training neural networks using pseudolabels generated
by traditional unsupervised algorithms on all samples. Gong
et al. [21] utilized pseudolabels derived from fuzzy clustering for
model ﬁne-tuning. However, due to the limitations of traditional
methods, there inevitably exists a certain degree of discrepancy
between pseudolabels and true labels. Particularly in scenarios
lacking labeled supervision, traditional unsupervised algorithms
struggle to effectively distinguish between genuinely anomalous
changes of interest and “pseudochanges” caused by shadows,
sensor registration errors, or alterations in atmospheric con-
ditions. This leads to networks being easily misled by noisy
labels during training, resulting in detection outcomes frequently
accompanied by a high number of false alarms, particularly in
complex urban environments. Li et al. [22] attempted to com-
pensate for this discrepancy by incorporating noise models into
the loss function, but the results were unsatisfactory. The core
challenge of such methods lies in the difﬁculty of ﬁltering out all
noisy labels through a single sample selection, signiﬁcantly un-
dermining the reliability of network training. To overcome these
limitations, Jiang et al. [23] proposed semisupervised methods
based on GANs. While these approaches signiﬁcantly reduced
dependence on labeled data, the unreliability of unlabeled data
still introduced interference into network training.
In recent years, diffusion models (DMs) have emerged as
cutting-edge generative models, introducing novel approaches
to the ﬁeld of hyperspectral image change detection (HSI-CD)
through their powerful denoising and feature extraction capabili-
ties. By progressively adding noise to data, DMs learn to recover
original data from noisy inputs, thereby generating new data
samples. In HSI-CD, Zhang et al. [24] pioneered the application
of DMs to this domain by proposing DiffUCD. This method,
based on the semantic correlation DM and cross-temporal con-
trastive learning, learns features insensitive to spectral variations
caused by environmental changes, achieving results compara-
ble to fully supervised methods. Furthermore, Wu et al. [25]
proposed the diffusion background dictionary method, which
essentially combines the DM with the low-rank representation
(LRR) models. By utilizing DM to extract key background
dictionary tensors from the LRR tensor, the method achieves
accurate anomaly detection (AD). Ma et al. [26] introduced
the background-suppressed diffusion model (BSDM) for hy-
perspectral AD. By designing pseudobackground noise, BSDM
leverages DMs to learn underlying background distributions, ef-
fectively suppressing complex background noise and enhancing
AD accuracy and generalization capabilities.
Although the aforementioned studies demonstrate that adopt-
ing the structure of DMs is an effective approach to addressing
the robustness challenges of HSI-CD or AD, directly applying
traditional DMs still faces signiﬁcant computational bottlenecks.
Existing generative approaches (e.g., DiffUCD and BSDM)
adhere to the standard diffusion paradigm [27], [28], relying
on cumbersome Markov chain time-step iterative sampling pro-
cesses (typically requiring hundreds or even thousands of itera-
tions) to extract features [29]. This high computational cost and
extremely slow inference speed severely limit their applicability
in real-time processing of massive HSI datasets. Therefore,
how to retain the powerful distribution modeling and noise
resistance advantages of generative models while overcoming
their inefﬁcient inference defects is a pressing challenge that
needs to be addressed.
To address the challenges in computational efﬁciency, reten-
tion of minor targets, and suppression of spurious variations,
we draw inspiration from the denoising score matching (DSM)
theory that underpins DMs [30]. We observe that the efﬁciency
of DMs hinges on their ability to compress high-dimensional
data into a compact latent space, where denoising and feature
reconstruction occur. This “perceptual compression” process is
fundamentally a form of manifold learning, effectively sepa-
rating high-frequency noise from core semantic information.
While standard DMs rely on iterative Markov chains to generate
diverse samples, the goal of anomaly CD is not to generate
diverse new samples, but to obtain feature representations that
are robust to background variations and sensitive to anomalies.
Therefore, we can abandon the random iterative process, re-
constructing it as a deterministic, one-step manifold projection
operation. We propose the efﬁcient latent denoising-inspired
network (ELDI-Net). Unlike previous approaches, ELDI-Net
does not directly employ standard diffusion generation. Instead,
it utilizes a Siamese autoencoder architecture constrained by
implicit denoising learning, conceptually translating the “noise
separation” mechanism of DMs into an efﬁcient one-step pro-
jection paradigm to construct a self-supervised anomaly CD
framework that simultaneously achieves extreme efﬁciency,
high robustness, and pixel-level accuracy.
The core innovation of the proposed ELDI-Net lies in recon-
structing the “iterative denoising” of traditional DMs into a “one-
step latent manifold projection,” while integrating spectral–
spatial attention and bidirectional alignment mechanisms. The
main contributions of this study are summarized as follows.

during forward projection, we embedded an efﬁcient channel

robust semantic-level denoising.

sensors. It utilizes the classical Diff-RX statistic measured on the
optimized latent feature Z, achieving higher area under the curve

II. M ETHODOLOGY
This section details the proposed ELDI-Net. Fig. 1 illustrates
the overall framework of ELDI-Net for HSI-CD. This frame-
work is built upon the OLMPF as its core backbone. Structurally,
the SACP is embedded directly into the Siamese encoder to
dynamically ﬁlter spectral redundancy before latent projection.
Subsequently, the bidirectional focused alignment mechanism
(BFAM) acts as a strong regularizer within the latent space, op-
timizing feature alignment through pseudolabel-guided implicit

semantic denoising. Finally, independent of the training phase,
a GCI strategy is applied during testing to eliminate directional
systematic noise.
A. Network Overview
Given two dual-temporal HSIs T1 ∈ RH×W×C and T2 ∈
RH×W×C , whereH, W, andC denote height, width, and num-
ber of spectral bands, respectively. ELDI-Net aims to learn pixel-
level difference feature representations under self-supervised
conditions. Unlike the cumbersome iterative sampling process
of traditional DMs, ELDI-Net employs a one-step deterministic
manifold projection to perform feature alignment and implicit
denoising reconstruction in the latent space directly.
First, under the proposed OLMPF, we construct a Siamese en-
coder, projector, and decoder with shared weights. The encoder
integrates the SACP module to mapT1 andT2 into latent features
Z1 and Z2, respectively, while the decoder is responsible for
noise-free background reconstruction, and the projector maps
these features into a dedicated alignment space. Subsequently,
cross-view prediction is performed using the predictor to align
with the projected features. To address the goal-oriented chal-
lenge in self-supervised learning, we introduce pseudolabels
based on preclassiﬁcation and compute a bidirectional focused
cosine similarity loss only within selected high-conﬁdence in-
variant regions. Finally, the GCI strategy fuses multiview in-
ference results in the latent space, and the Diff-RX algorithm
generates high-precision difference maps.
To make the whole pipeline easier to follow, the process can
be summarized into following three sequential stages.
1) Calibration and projection: The Siamese encoder
(equipped with SACP) compresses the dual-temporal in-
puts into noise-ﬁltered, low intrinsic-dimension latent fea-
tures.
2) Implicit denoising and alignment: The Siamese decoder
reconstructs the background, while BFAM strongly con-
strains the latent space to align invariant features and
suppress pseudovariations.
3) Robust inference: During testing, the GCI strategy fuses
multiview latent features, which are then fed into the Diff-
RX detector to output the ﬁnal anomaly change map. The
overall network architecture is shown in Fig. 1.

time complexity to a constant-order O(1) with respect to the

dimensions and band count of the input data, this single-step
deterministic projection avoids redundant gradient computa-

computational bottlenecks introduced by Markov chains [32].
By jointly optimizing the reconstruction error and subsequent
alignment loss, OLMPF achieves a substantial leap in inference
speed while preserving the generative model’s powerful feature
extraction capabilities and ensuring feature manifold accuracy.
This makes it perfectly suited for hyperspectral AD, which
demands both high precision and real-time performance.

Due to directional variations during hyperspectral sensor

It is worth noting that a 1:1 equal weighting is adopted here.

the background manifold reconstruction nor the invariant feature
alignment dominates the optimization process.
Through joint optimization, the reconstruction loss ensures
that latent features fully capture the original spectral infor-
mation, while the alignment loss compels the network to dis-
regard irrelevant background noise. This mechanism enables

conditions, signiﬁcantly enhancing the model’s robustness in

This robust self-supervised learning performance against po-
tential low-quality or insufﬁcient pseudolabels is theoretically
guaranteed by two core mechanisms in our architecture.
First, the OLMPF acts as an information bottleneck. Anoma-
lous changes (which might be mistakenly included as unchanged
pseudolabels due to CV A limitations or relaxed Otsu thresholds)
are statistically sparse. The shared encoder is compelled to
learn the dominant continuous background manifold, effectively
treating these sparse, erroneous labels as high-frequency noise
and ﬁltering them out during the projection process.
Second, the BFAM explicitly prevents the network from
overﬁtting to corrupted pseudolabels. Even if a changed pixel is
erroneously masked as “unchanged,” its heterogeneous feature
representation across the dual-temporal inputs makes cross-
prediction extremely difﬁcult. The focal cosine loss dynamically
adjusts the gradients, preventing these inconsistent outliers from
dominating the alignment process. Therefore, ELDI-Net does
not merely memorize the CV A-generated labels but utilizes them
as rough anchors to independently discover the true intrinsic
background manifold.
IV . DISCUSSION
While the experimental results comprehensively demonstrate
the superior performance of the proposed ELDI-Net framework,
several broader implications, underlying limitations, and future
research directions warrant further discussion.
A. Methodological Implications for Hyperspectral Detection
Generative models, particularly DMs, have recently shown
immense potential in remote sensing feature representation.
However, their reliance on Markov chain iterative sampling
inherently contradicts the strict real-time processing demands
of HACD tasks. The primary methodological contribution of
ELDI-Net lies in successfully bridging this gap. By theoretically
grounding our design in DSM, ELDI-Net distills the powerful
generative representation capabilities of DMs into a determin-
istic, one-step projection framework. This paradigm shift—
extracting a low intrinsic-dimension, noise-robust semantic
manifold without iterative steps—demonstrates that high-
ﬁdelity generative feature alignment can be achieved in real-
time, offering a new perspective for deploying diffusion-inspired
architectures in time-sensitive hyperspectral applications.
B. Limitations and Future Directions
Despite the substantial improvements in both efﬁciency and
accuracy, the current ELDI-Net framework exhibits certain
27904 IEEE JOURNAL OF SELECTED TOPICS IN APPLIED EARTH OBSERV ATIONS AND REMOTE SENSING, VOL. 19, 2026
structural limitations that provide clear avenues for future re-
search. First, as noted in the experimental analysis, ELDI-Net
currently functions primarily as an advanced feature repre-
sentation and alignment network. The generation of the ﬁnal
anomaly change maps still relies on a classical backend detector
(e.g., Diff-RX). While the high-quality latent representations
from ELDI-Net signiﬁcantly maximize the backend detector’s
potential, this disjointed pipeline prevents the entire framework
from being optimized in a fully end-to-end manner. Future work
will focus on designing an integrated, learnable detection head
that can be jointly optimized with the latent projection process,
further streamlining the detection pipeline. Second, the current
framework is tailored exclusively for hyperspectral imagery. In
complex real-world scenarios, leveraging multimodal remote
sensing data (e.g., fusing hyperspectral data with LiDAR or
SAR) can provide complementary structural information, fur-
ther reducing false alarms caused by extreme illumination or
topological changes. Extending the ELDI-Net architecture to
accommodate and align multimodal dual-temporal inputs rep-
resents a promising trajectory for our future investigations.
V. C ONCLUSION
This article proposes an ELDI-Net for pixel-level self-
supervised HACD. Addressing the computational bottleneck
of traditional generative models, we design an OLMPF that
reconstructs the cumbersome Markov chain iterative denoising
in DMs into a single deterministic manifold projection at full
resolution. This paradigm shift achieves a leap in inference
efﬁciency (reaching 42 FPS) while preserving the noise-resilient
modeling advantages of latent space, effectively alleviating the
real-time processing challenge of DMs. To address feature learn-
ing under self-supervision, we introduce SACP , which dynami-
cally suppresses spectral redundancy to ensure precise retention
of critical features in small targets. Simultaneously, the BFAM
combines an improved focus cosine loss function with twin
cross-prediction and pseudolabel guidance to achieve implicit
semantic denoising, effectively suppressing pseudovariation ar-
tifacts caused by shadows and registration errors. Furthermore,
the GCI strategy introduced during inference statistically miti-
gates directional systematic noise from sensors. Extensive ex-
periments across three hyperspectral datasets demonstrate that
ELDI-Net not only achieves superior or highly competitive
detection accuracy across the evaluated complex scenarios but
also exhibits signiﬁcant computational efﬁciency advantages,
substantially reducing the inference latency compared to tradi-
tional iterative DMs.
Despite these promising results, we objectively acknowledge
that the overall performance of ELDI-Net still intrinsically relies
on the initial pseudolabels generated by traditional CV A and
Otsu thresholding. Although our robust architecture exhibits
strong tolerance to label noise, extremely low-quality initial
pseudolabels under severe imaging degradation could still bound
the ﬁnal alignment accuracy. Future work will focus on ex-
ploring fully end-to-end self-supervised representation learning
mechanisms and dynamic pseudolabel self-correction strategies
to further reduce the reliance on hand-crafted prior informa-
tion, thereby extending the method’s generalizability to more
extreme, fully unsupervised environments.
