ABSTRACT
Correct classification of crop layers is crucial for crop growth tracking and estimating yields, but existing 
methods depends heavily on the completeness of data and ground truth labels. Self-supervised learning (SSL) 
enables crop mapping when data is limited. However, current SSL approaches fail to fully integrate information 
across spatiotemporal dimensions and lack the ability to learn and represent consistent ground object features. 
To this end, a self-supervised crop mapping framework is proposed, which integrates adaptive k-means clus-
tering, graph convolution, and global spatiotemporal attention (SAKG). This framework reduces reliance on 
labeled data through contrastive learning and achieves implicit semantic grouping of crop spatiotemporal fea-
tures using adaptive K-means clustering, graph convolution, and self-attention mechanisms, enhancing model 
robustness and representation of complex data while mitigating sample imbalance issues. Three experiments 
(cross spatial, cross temporal, cross Spatiotemporal) demonstrate that SAKG outperforms existing models in most 
transfer scenarios. Furthermore, t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding(t-SNE) visualization verifies its 
effective reduction of spectral differences between the source and target domains, achieving consistent repre-
sentation of crop features. Overall, this method can effectively utilize existing satellite data and labels to achieve 
large-scale crop mapping. The code can be obtained here: https://github.com/LijianXie/SAKG-net.
1. Introduction
Under the twin pressures of a growing global population and 
frequent extreme weather events, food security is facing significant 
challenges (Yang et al. 2023b). According to the 2024 World Population 
Outlook, the world’s population will continue to growing and may even 
exceed 10.2 billion by the end of this century. (https://news.un.org/zh 
/story/2024/07/1129876). This exponential population growth will 
lead to a surge in global food demand, posing a severe threat to the 
sustainability of 21st-century agricultural systems. For this reason, there 
is an urgent requirement to build a precise and trustworthy agricultural 
monitoring system to secure global food security and achieve sustain-
able development goals (Wang et al. 2025).
It is noteworthy that, due to diverse planting and management 
practices across different regions, land cover can undergo drastic 
changes from bare soil to full vegetation cover within hours, posing 
difficulties for traditional survey methods (Huang et al. 2022).Under 
these circumstances, remote sensing technologies, with their ability to 
conduct large-scale, high-frequency observations, can effectively 
monitor crop growth status and areas across different stages, progres-
sively supplanting conventional survey methods as the main technique 
for crop type investigations (Wen et al. 2025).
However, most crop mapping efforts are still affected by the avail-
ability of effective observational data and interpretation methods. For 
instance, optical remote sensing data is highly susceptible to weather 
conditions, making it difficult to obtain complete observational data for 
describing crop phenological characteristics, thus limiting the ability of 
the model to acquire crop feature (You et al. 2023). Many studies 
indicate that diverse source data can relatively comprehensively reveal 
crop growth cycles and their spectral response characteristics, enabling 
classification tasks (Shen et al. 2022). However, due to feature redun-
dancy and the large volume of diverse source data, existing model

spectral, and phenological features, limiting the capacity of the model to 
describe the intrinsic and extrinsic properties of crops (Alami Machichi 
et al. 2023). This is particularly challenging in regions with a variety of 
crop types, uneven farmland areas, and diverse farming methods, 
especially when it comes to identifying specific crops such as soybeans 
and corn (Qiu et al. 2025).
Compared to traditional approaches, deep learning demonstrates 
notable strengths in feature representation and learning (Zhang et al. 
2020). For instance, Gao et al. proposed the Phenological Horizon 
Attention Transformer (PHAT) model based on the transformer to ach-
ieve rapid and accurate extraction of multiple crops over large areas, 
yielding an overall accuracy of 0.90 (Gao et al. 2025). Wang et al. 
proposed a lightweight multi-level CNN-Transformer network (MCTNet) 
that maintains high accuracy with minimal parameters and memory 
usage(Wang et al. 2024).The primary reason is that the Transformer 
architecture abandons traditional recurrent computations, achieving 
global context modeling through self-attention mechanisms while 
maintaining the advantages of parallel computing and improving the 
ability of the model to identify long-term spatiotemporal dependencies 
(Vaswani et al. 2017).
However, existing Transformer-based studies often focus on tempo-
ral feature extraction, neglecting the synergistic evolutionary patterns of 
crop types across spatial–temporal dimensions. To address this issue, 
existing studies have integrated CNN with Vision Transformer (ViT), 
achieving the extraction and representation of spatiotemporal features, 
and triggering a wave of research enthusiasm in remote sensing (Yan 
et al. 2023). For instance, Jamali and others who put forward a CNN-ViT 
that effectively achieved monitoring and mapping of Blueberry scorch 
virus (Jamali et al. 2025). Notably, ViT and its derivative models 
commonly face challenges in remote sensing scenarios, such as the 
inability to extract spatiotemporal feature relationships from optical 
remote sensing data with missing values and issues related to high 
computational complexity. Although Liu et al. proposed the Swin 
Transformer, which significantly improves model computational effi-
ciency through a hierarchical window-based self-attention mechanism, 
the aforementioned core challenges remain unresolved (Liu et al. 2021).
Given the complex spatial–temporal correlations and dynamics of 
time-series data, exploring spatial–temporal missing values and their 
inter-representation relationships to achieve primary feature charac-
terization has become an important and challenging research direction 
(Feng et al. 2024; Pelletier et al. 2019). Even though supervised deep 
learning approaches have addressed some of the aforementioned chal-
lenges and demonstrated outstanding performance in crop mapping, 
they remain limited by the availability of image data quantity and 
quality. unsupervised methods can operate without labels, they often 
fail to effectively capture the intricate spatial–temporal dependencies 
within the data (Wang et al. 2022c). Unlike supervised learning that 
depends on annotation data and complete time series data, as well as 
unsupervised methods that may ignore these complex relationships, SSL 
starts from the intrinsic structure of the data itself and learns its feature 
representation forms (Gui et al. 2024). Specifically, SSL primarily ex-
tracts intrinsic supervisory information from massive unlabeled data 
through subsidiary task, and utilizes this constructed supervised infor-
mation to train the net, thereby learning feature representations that are 
valuable for downstream tasks (Xu et al. 2024b). Contrastive and 
generative approaches are the two most prevalent paradigms in current 
image self-supervised methods (Wang et al. 2022b). Contrastive 
learning approaches learn representation by minimize the distances 
among sample pairs in the embedded space, whereas generative ap-
proaches learn representation by rebuilding inputs or generating data 
distributions (Ohri and Kumar 2021). This technique has garnered 
increasing attention in remote sensing temporal processing tasks, as 
exemplified by Qin, who introduce a Spatiotemporal Collaborative 
Learning Network (STCLN) to spatiotemporal expressions, achieving 
outstanding crop type mapping capabilities under label-scarce 
conditions (Qin et al. 2025).
Although current research has demonstrated that self-supervised 
learning can be effectively applied to representation learning for 
various data types, those approaches often overlook the latent graph 
structures and latent space distance relationships in multivariate time- 
series data, limiting their representation capabilities (Jin et al. 2024).
In contrast, Graph Neural Networks (GNN), as a learning architec-
ture designed specifically for graph representation, can more effectively 
capture complex spatial–temporal features in multivariate time-series 
data by generating random subgraphs as samples and constructing 
corresponding adjacency matrices for each sample (Yang et al. 2023a). 
For example, Ding et. al. Based on the self-training strategy, by intro-
ducing spectral-space transformation and designing a local maintain 
graph embedded encoder and autocorrelation learning smoothing 
module, the robustness of remote sensing data clustering is greatly 
improved (Ding et al. 2022a). In addition, some studies, while 
addressing this issue, have achieved high clustering accuracy and low 
complexity in hyperspectral images by introducing a low-pass graph 
denoising mechanism and a dual-branch graph contrastive learning 
module; however, when handling highly dynamic time-series data, due 
to their graph feature construction methods, they may still face signifi-
cant computational burdens, necessitating further enhancement of joint 
spatiotemporal modeling capabilities to improve prediction accuracy 
and computational efficiency (Ding et al. 2025b; Wang et al. 2019). As 
an unsupervised method, K-means can automatically cluster pixels 
based on spectral features without prior labels and segment images into 
different categories. Combining it with graph networks not only enables 
deep exploration of dynamic spectral and spatial differences in crops 
across various growth stages but also reduces the computational 
complexity of graph networks, improving computational efficiency. As 
proposed by Ding et al., an Adaptive Homomorphic Structure Graph 
Clustering (AHSGC) method effectively encodes spatial structure infor-
mation and represents latent features while reducing complexity (Ding 
et al. 2025a).
Although the aforementioned studies have demonstrated that the 
introduction of clustering and graph convolution can learn super pixel- 
level spectral-spatial features, reduce the number of graph nodes pro-
cessed by subsequent networks, and significantly enhance feature rep-
resentation and computational efficiency through self-training strategies 
and spectral-spatial transformations, challenges in crop mapping persist 
due to the lack of prior knowledge, high spectral variability, and high 
data dimensionality (Ding et al. 2022b).
Therefore, this study integrates the strengths of the aforementioned 
methods, constructing a self-supervised learning crop mapping frame-
work based on Sentinel-2 time-series imagery, utilizing graph convolu-
tion, k-means clustering, and the self-attention mechanism of the 
transformer model. The key contribution to our research is: 
1) Established a contrastive SSL framework for crop feature represen-
tation, addressing the strong dependence of traditional supervised 
learning on the completeness of remote sensing data and labeled 
data, reducing feature redundancy in high-dimensional time-series 
data, and enhancing model generalization capability.
2) The K-means algorithm is improved to enable adaptive clustering. 
Integrated it with graph networks to achieve implicit semantic 
grouping of crop features, enhancing the model’s robustness in 
identifying small-sample categories.
3) Through the combination of the self-attention module and an 
adaptive feature fusion mechanism, the capacity to capture long- 
term spatiotemporal dependencies of crop features and express 
complex data is improved.

2. Study area and datasets
2.1. Study area
The research spans multiple regions in China and the United States of 
America (USA), with site selection fully considering the importance of 
corn and soybean production as well as the diversity of climate and 
agricultural conditions (Fig. 1). China selected Kedong (KD) County, 
Heilongjiang (HLJ) (temperate continental monsoon climatic region, 
yearly average 0–3 ◦C, precipitation 400–600 mm) and Si(S) County, 
Anhui (AH) (warm temperate semi-humid monsoon climate zone, yearly 
average 14–15 ◦C, precipitation 700–900 mm). The USA selected six 
counties in the Corn Belt: Grant and Stevens Counties in Minnesota 
(MN), Kossuth County in Iowa (IA), Iroquois County in Illinois (IL), and 
Jay and Randolph Counties in Indiana (IN), with temperate continental 
climate, yearly average 8–12 ◦C, precipitation 700–1000 mm, and flat 
terrain. Since some areas belong to the same state, they are abbreviated 
in the following text (MN_Grant_Stevens → MN).

3. Method
Although self-supervised learning can be effectively applied to rep-
resentation learning for various data types, the limitations of custom- 
trained feature extraction hinder the model’s ability to explore feature 
dependencies across different dimensions. Thus, this study enhances the 
K-means clustering and integrates it with graph convolution into a self- 
supervised framework, while incorporating a spatiotemporal self- 
attention mechanism to achieve robust feature extraction and repre-
sentation, tackling the model’s dependence on data completeness and 
feature redundancy in high-dimensional time-series data. The process is 
show in Fig. 3.
3.1. SAKG for pre-training
In this phase, due to the significant influence of factors such as 
illumination, cloud cover, and seasonal variations on time-series optical 
data, we employed data augmentation strategies to simulate these 
changes. This approach increases data diversity, enhancing the ability of 
the model to learn invariant inherent features of data (Maharana et al. 
2022). Furthermore, to achieve effective feature extraction, we designed 
a feature encoder branch with a self-attention mechanism and a clus-
tering graph convolution branch. By leveraging diagonal and off- 
diagonal loss terms, robust feature support is provided for down-
stream tasks (Zbontar et al. 2021). Our pre-training framework is illus-
trated in Fig. 4.

3.1.1. Self-attention mechanism encoder feature extraction branch
The feature encoder with a self-attention mechanism is designed to 
comprehensively captures the dynamic changes in sequence data, 
overcoming the limitations of missing contextual information and 
enhancing global expression capabilities of features. The encoder and 
self-attention mechanism serve as the primary branch for feature 
extraction in our approach, where the encoder comprises four conv 
layers. Each conv layer has a kernel size of 3 × 3, a step size of 1, padding 
of 1, and includes max-pooling and ReLU activation functions. As shown 
in Fig. 4, the encoder initially applies convolution operations to the data- 
augmented images [X1, X2, … XN], producing the feature ca1; Then, the 
computed features undergo pooling operations and ReLU activation 
functions. Subsequently, these features are fused with the features ga1 
generated by the clustering graph convolution through an adaptive 
feature fusion (ADF) module, and processed by the self-attention module 
to obtain the feature input ca2 to the next convolutional layer. After four 
convolutional processes, the features are projected and mapped to the 
feature Za. To enhance self-supervised feature extraction, the computed 
feature Za is subjected to a consistency constraint with the feature Zb 
derived from another augmented image, resulting in the loss L oss1. 
Then, through gradient backpropagation, the model parameters are 
updated, achieving the training of model parameters.
3.1.2. Adaptive clustering graph convolution branch
The clustering graph convolution branch primarily consists of 
adaptive K-means clustering and a graph convolution module (Fig. 5). 
This branch is designed to enhance crop feature representation, mitigate 
the interference of complex pixel relationships on feature extraction, 
and ensure stable representations in self-supervised learning. Due to the 
inherent nature of graph convolution, computational cost increases 
sharply with the node count (Tang et al. 2021). To address this, we 
adopted a process of first applying clustering to reduce computational 
load, followed by graph convolution for feature extraction. Additionally, 
since the pixel value distribution of raw time-series optical data may be 
highly dispersed or correlated (e.g., redundancy across multiple bands), 
and K-means relies on Euclidean distance, noise and low discrimina-
bility in the raw data can disrupt the convergence of cluster centers, 
potentially leading to inaccurate cluster assignments or excessive 
computational complexity. To mitigate this, we first pass the data- 
augmented images [X1, X2, … XN] through a 3 × 3 convolutional layer 
to transform the low-level pixel values of the raw data into higher-level 
feature representations before adaptive clustering, enhancing the se-
mantic expressiveness of the data. The results of clustering are subse-
quently subjected to graph convolution to derive the feature g. 
Furthermore, to ensure multi-scale information extraction and syner-
gistic integration with the features extracted by the encoder, we applied 
four graph convolution operations during the graph convolution 
process, yielding [g1,g2,g3,g4].
3.1.3. Self-attention mechanism
In the crop classification task employing time-series multispectral 
remote sensing imagery, the spatial relationships between pixels and the 
spectral features across different time steps are critical to classification 
performance (Wang et al. 2022a). Therefore, a self-attention mechanism 
(Fig. 6) was designed in our model to enhance the crop classification task 
utilizing time-series multispectral remote sensing imagery. This mech-
anism generated Key(K), Query(Q), and Value(V) through 1 × 1 
convolution and uses scaled dot-product attention to compute dynamic 
weights between pixels, thereby capturing long-range spatial 
dependencies.

features to generate cluster-enhanced features, providing more 
discriminative input for the graph convolutional network, thereby

3.2. Supervised fine-tuning
The supervised image classification training step (Fig. 8), we 
removed the pre-training loss function L oss1 and data augmentation, 
while keeping the remaining components consistent with the self- 
supervised encoder feature extraction phase. Additionally, we intro-

model to perform the corresponding crop classification task after fine-

3.2.1. Decoder
In the image classification task, the decoder is made up of four 
upsampling layers, each incorporating a self-attention mechanism, skip 
connections, and a ReLU activation function. Initially, the encoder’s 
features are upsampled, then combined with the encoder-extracted 
features through skip connections to maintain information complete-
ness. Thereafter, normalization operations and the self-attention 
mechanism are applied to gradually recover resolution while preser-
ving global contextual integrity.

4.2. Normalized difference vegetation index comparison at study area
To illustrate the impact of spectral feature differences of crops across 
different regions and times on the model, we plotted the temporal NDVI 
curves for corn, soybeans, and other crops (Fig. 9.). Specifically, around 
100 days into the growing period, spectral features begin to emerge, but 
the differences are not yet pronounced; between 150 and 300 days, 
NDVI curves display more distinct crop-specific characteristics, while 
after 300 days, the spectra of different crops start to overlap, and 
spectral feature differences diminish. Spectral feature overlap is pri-
marily due to similar leaf structures and photosynthetic characteristics 
among crops of the same family, particularly during early crop growth 
and senescence periods, where consistent patterns of higher near- 
infrared reflection and lower red reflection are observed. The differ-
ences in spectral features are attributed to the heterogeneity of pheno-
logical development stages, seasonal variations, short-term disturbance 
events in the temporal dimension, and environmental factors such as 
climate, soil, crop type/variety, and field management in the spatial 
dimension. The NDVI curves indicate that variations in crop phenolog-
ical stages across locations and years may affect the model’s feature 
extraction capabilities. Therefore, developing models to learn the pri-
mary feature representations of ground objects is highly important.

5. Discussion
5.1. Ablation study
To assess the efficacy of adaptive clustering graph, adaptive feature 
fusion, self-attention mechanism, and pre-training methods, we con-
ducted ablative tests on different sets of components using a collection of 
data from two counties in Iowa in 2021. As shown in Table 4, By com-
parison of Ablation 1,3, it is found that adding the adaptive clustering 
graph convolution module improved mF1 by 1.89 %. Compared to only 
adding the self-attention mechanism (Ablation 2), the improvement was 
greater, with mIoU increasing by 3.09 %. Compared with Ablation 6, the 
poor performance of the self-attention mechanism (Ablation 3) is mainly 
due to the inability to dynamically weight features from different 
sources or scales, resulting in unreliable features being overweighted or 
important features being overweighted, which affects model perfor-
mance. By comparison of Ablation 1,5, the overall performance 
improved significantly due to consistency training enhancing the 
representation capability of ground object features. Overall, the joint 
addition of the adaptive clustering graph convolution module and pre- 
training steps showed significant performance improvements, with 
mIoU improving by 3.79 % (comparing Ablation 1 and 5). We further 
compare with simpler baseline models (e.g., standard ResNet or ViT) 
that do not contain any of the proposed modules to better understand 
the absolute performance improvement brought by the entire SAKG 
framework. The relevant results and discussions are detailed in Appen-
dix C.

5.3. Advantages and limitations
Although the proposed SAKG demonstrates good performance in 
crop type classification, performance degradation persists in cross- 
regional and cross-temporal transfers. As shown in Table 3 and 
Fig. 13, the model trained in the Anhui region exhibits significantly 
reduced performance after transfer validation. The primary reasons are 
twofold: first, there are substantial differences in cultivation practices 
between the U.S. and Chinese regions; second, the large latitudinal span 
during model application leads to significant differences in crop 
phenological information that are difficult to align, resulting in perfor-
mance decline.
To address this, future research will select regions with similar crop 
growing seasons and closer latitudes for model transfer. Moreover, 
future work will further integrate the use of multi-source and multi- 
modal data to improve the robustness of the model in different Agro-
ecological zones (Boehm and Liesenberg 2024).Refine the scope of the 
“other” category to achieve accurate feature representation and classi-
fication. These enhancements align with the growing need for scalable, 
adaptable models in precision agriculture, particularly as global agri-
cultural systems face increasing pressures from climate change and land- 
use shifts. Additionally, incorporating emerging technologies, such as 
advanced remote sensing or AI-driven decision support systems, could 
further strengthen model generalizability and support sustainable agri-
cultural policies worldwide. These improvements are critical for

6. Conclusion
To mitigate the reliance of existing crop mapping approaches on 
extensive labeling data and complete image sequences, and the effi-
ciency decline caused by environmental differences in cross-regional 
and cross-temporal applications, this paper presents the SSL frame-
work SAKG. This method has the following advantages: first, it improves 
the ability of the model to learn and represent consistent characteristics 
of ground objects through self-supervised learning of the data’s intrinsic 
representation forms; second, it achieves spatiotemporal feature asso-
ciation of ground objects via adaptive clustering graph convolution, 
learning the distributions differences among the target domain and the 
source domain to reduce the need for labels. Additionally, to thoroughly 
validate the superiority of this method, we conducted transfer experi-
ments across spatial, temporal, and spatiotemporal dimensions. In most 
experiments, this model’s performance surpassed that of other models. 
Furthermore, by using t-SNE to visualize, the distribution of the feature 
distributions before and after domain transfer, we confirmed that this 
method effectively learns spectral feature distribution differences and 
enhances crop separability.
