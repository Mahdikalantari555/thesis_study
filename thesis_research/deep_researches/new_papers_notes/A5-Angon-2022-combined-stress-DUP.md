## Summary
PRISMA systematic review of plant responses to combined drought and salinity stress, screening 536 records down to 30 included studies. The review shows that combined salinity–drought stress impairs growth, photosynthesis, ionic balance, and oxidative balance more severely than either stress alone. Drought inhibits photosynthesis more strongly, whereas salinity drives ionic imbalance and root-biomass effects more strongly. Antioxidant systems do not scale proportionally under combined stress, indicating non-additive interactive effects rather than simple stress sum.

## Methods / Approach
- PRISMA systematic review and meta-analytic synthesis
- Literature screening: 536 records → 30 primary studies
- Thematic extraction across growth, photosynthesis, ionic balance, oxidative balance, and antioxidant responses
- Comparison of single-stress versus combined-stress effects

## Key quantitative results
- Screening cascade: 536 records → 30 studies included
- Combined stress worse than either alone across growth, photosynthesis, ionic, and oxidative endpoints
- Drought relatively stronger on photosynthesis; salinity relatively stronger on ionic imbalance and root biomass
- Antioxidant capacity does not scale under combined stress (non-additive interaction)
