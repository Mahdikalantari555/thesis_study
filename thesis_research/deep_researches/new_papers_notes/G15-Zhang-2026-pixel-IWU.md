# A novel framework for pixel-wise estimation of irrigation water use by integrating remote sensing and reanalysis data

**Authors:** Zhang, L., Che, T., Zhang, K., Zheng, D., & Li, X.  
**Year:** 2026  
**Venue:** Agricultural Water Management, 323, 110077  
**DOI / arXiv:** 10.1016/j.agwat.2025.110077  
**Local PDF:** 1-s2.0-S0378377425007917-main.pdf  
**Type:** methods  
**Thesis relevance:** MEDIUM  

## Summary
The paper proposes a framework for pixel-wise irrigation water use (IWU) estimation that couples soil water balance models (RSM/SSM) with satellite soil moisture, evapotranspiration (ET), and reanalysis data. An ensemble approach produces 1 km IWU estimates for China over 2001–2020 and is evaluated at the prefecture scale.

## Methods / Architecture
- Soil water balance models: RSM and SSM.
- Inputs: satellite soil moisture, satellite ET, reanalysis products.
- Ensemble of models at 1 km resolution.
- Study domain: China, 2001–2020.
- Validation scale: prefecture-level.

## Key quantitative results
- Prefecture-level R²: 0.72–0.90.
- RMSE: 0.55–0.66 km³/yr.
- Observed increasing IWU trend over the study period.

## Relevance to thesis
Provides arid irrigation covariates and a validation context for regional water-use dynamics; useful as supporting background for arid crop-stress monitoring rather than as a stress-detection method itself.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Framework targets water-use accounting, not direct plant-stress identification.
- 1 km resolution is coarse relative to field-scale stress monitoring.
- Opportunity: use IWU trends as contextual features for EO-FM stress classifiers in irrigated arid zones.
