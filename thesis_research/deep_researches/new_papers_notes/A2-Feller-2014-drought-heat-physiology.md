## Summary
Narrative review of drought and high-temperature effects on crop physiology, covering stomatal regulation, cuticular wax, aquaporins, Rubisco activase heat sensitivity, reactive oxygen species (ROS), proline accumulation, dehydrins, carbon/nitrogen transport, and senescence. The authors show that heat and drought can act oppositely on stomata, and emphasize the importance of recovery stages after stress release. Canopy and leaf temperature responses are framed as integrative physiological outcomes under concurrent climatic stress, with direct implications for interpreting thermal remote sensing signals in hot agricultural regions.

## Methods / Approach
- Narrative literature synthesis of drought and heat physiology in agronomically important plants
- Thematic organization by process: stomata, wax, aquaporins, Rubisco activase, ROS, compatible solutes (proline), dehydrins, transport, senescence
- Emphasis on interactive (versus single-factor) drought × heat effects and recovery phases

## Key quantitative results
- Sun-exposed leaf temperature often 5–10°C above shaded leaves (review-cited, Bern 2003 example)
- Leaf temperature can exceed 40°C in late afternoon under combined heat and drought conditions (Bern 2003 example)
- Other quantitative details are process-level rather than meta-analytic effect sizes

## Relevance to thesis
Concurrent drought and heat are the dominant confounders for Khuzestan summer crop monitoring, so canopy temperature may reflect atmospheric heat demand rather than only soil water deficit. This paper motivates inclusion of meteorological co-drivers (air temperature, vapor pressure deficit) alongside thermal features when labeling crop stress. The opposing stomatal responses to heat versus drought further justify probabilistic or multi-class stress labels rather than a single binary water-stress index. Low remote-sensing and machine-learning content means the paper functions as physiological prior knowledge, not as an RS method source.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Include meteorological co-drivers explicitly in stress labels and feature sets to avoid confounding heat with drought
- Low RS/ML content relative to thesis methods; use mainly for physiological justification of confounders
- Opportunities for multi-temporal models that separate heat-driven from water-driven canopy heating
