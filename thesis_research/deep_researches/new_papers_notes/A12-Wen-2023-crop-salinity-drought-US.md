# Evaluating Crop-Specific Responses to Salinity and Drought Stress from Remote Sensing

**Authors:** Wen, W., Timmermans, J., Chen, Q., van Bodegom, P.M.
**Year:** 2023

**DOI / ID:** 10.1016/j.jag.2023.103438

## Summary
Assesses tolerances and vulnerabilities of crops to combined drought + salinity stress across plant functional types under real-life settings in the United States. Five traits evaluate the impacts of drought and salinity tolerance on a multitude of crops from March to October. Dominant stress, onset of combined vs individual effects, and crop-specific responses are mapped. Stress impacts strongly depend on time (within-season timing), and crops are more sensitive to combined salinity + drought than to either individual stress.

## Methods / Approach
- Five functional traits from satellite observations across US croplands
- Dominant-stress mapping and onset detection of combined vs individual stress
- Crop-specific tolerance/vulnerability comparison across plant functions

## Key quantitative results
- Combined salinity+drought sensitivity > individual stresses (consistent with Wen 2022 NL findings)
- Strong time dependence of stress impacts (March–October window)
- Crop-specific tolerances differ across plant functional types

## Relevance to thesis
Together with Wen 2021 (review) and Wen 2022 (NL validation), this paper forms the **closest existing research program to the thesis core problem** — concurrent drought × salinity from satellite traits — now upscaled to a continental, multi-crop setting. For Khuzestan sugarcane it provides: the tolerance-covariate idea (crop salt tolerance modulates spectral response, echoing Smanov 2026), timing as a first-class variable (supports temporal sequence modeling), and the combined > individual sensitivity result as direct motivation for mixed-stress labels. The trilogy remains **statistical/ANOVA-based** — deep temporal disentanglement, SSL, and XAI are absent, so the thesis niche stays open.

## Gaps / opportunities
- No predictive deep model; trait-stress relationships remain descriptive
- No sugarcane; US crop mix (comparative physiology, not arid irrigated tropics)
- Combined-stress detection, not disentanglement: no separation of water vs salinity contributions per pixel/season
