## Summary
Review of multi- and hyperspectral thermal infrared (TIR) remote sensing for crop water-stress detection, situating TIR alongside solar-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) and VNIR/SWIR optical approaches. The paper catalogs the multitude of plant responses to water stress and warns that co-occurring stresses confound single-response detection strategies. Sensor coverage includes EnMAP, PRISMA, FLEX, and planned SBG/HyspIRI-class missions. The central conclusion is that multi-sensor synergy is required for robust crop water-stress detection because no single spectral domain captures all relevant plant responses.

## Methods / Approach
- Review of TIR multi-/hyperspectral physical principles for crop water-stress detection
- Comparison with SIF and VNIR/SWIR optical stress indicators
- Survey of current and planned sensor capabilities (EnMAP, PRISMA, FLEX, SBG/HyspIRI-class)
- Analysis of confounding by co-occurring stresses on single-response detection designs

## Relevance to thesis
This paper supplies the water-stress physics foundation for positioning Landsat TIRS as a multi-year auxiliary thermal source alongside primary optical SSL/Mamba workflows. Its multi-sensor synergy argument underpins the thesis design of fusing thermal features with deep optical sequence models rather than relying on TIR alone. For irrigated arid Khuzestan, co-occurring salinity, heat, and water deficit make single-response detection unreliable—exactly the confounding regime the thesis targets. Resolution and overpass limits of current TIR systems also justify treating thermal as auxiliary and compensating with high-cadence optical data. The planned SBG/HyspIRI-class mission context frames future operational opportunities for thermal multi-sensor fusion.

## Gaps / opportunities
- Fuse thermal and optical domains explicitly rather than treating TIR as a standalone stress index source
- Address spatial-resolution and overpass-frequency limits of current TIR sensors
- Opportunities for multi-temporal thermal–optical sequence models under concurrent abiotic stresses
